How to Handle a Review From Someone Who Claims They Were a Customer

RB
ReviewBoostPRO Team
• August 14, 2026 • 9 min read

Picture this: It is a busy Saturday evening at your dental clinic in Bandra, Mumbai, or your multi-speciality restaurant in Koramangala, Bangalore. You check your Google Business Profile notification bell, expecting another happy customer sharing kind words. Instead, you see a stinging 1-star review from a profile you have never seen before, written by someone claiming they visited your establishment yesterday and received terrible, rude service.

Your heart rate spikes. You search your appointment registers, your billing software, and your WhatsApp chat logs. There is no record of this person. No bill generated, no appointment booked, no consultation fee paid of ₹500 or ₹2000. They are a complete stranger. But to prospective customers scrolling through your listing on their smartphones, that review looks entirely real.

Dealing with unverified, fake, or fraudulent reviews is one of the most frustrating challenges Indian local business owners face. Whether it is a disgruntled competitor in Pune trying to drag down your ratings, a malicious prankster in Delhi, or simply a case of mistaken identity, how you respond can make or break your local reputation.

The Psychology Behind Fake "Customer" Reviews

Why do people leave reviews claiming they patronised a business when they never did? In the hyper-competitive local markets across India—from bustling retail hubs in Chennai to growing healthcare clusters in Hyderabad—reputation is digital currency. A drop of even 0.3 stars on Google can significantly reduce footfall and direct calls.

Unverified negative reviews generally fall into three categories:

  • Competitor Sabotage: Rival businesses hiring third-party agencies or using dummy Google accounts to post generic complaints about hygiene, staff behavior, or billing transparency.
  • Mistaken Identity: The reviewer genuinely visited a shop with a similar name in the same locality (for instance, mistaking one pharmacy for another down the same lane in Karol Bagh).
  • Extortion Attempts: Occasionally, bad actors leave a scathing 1-star review and subsequently DM your business phone number demanding money or free services to take it down.
Critical Warning Never lash out in anger or type an aggressive, sarcastic response. Future customers read your replies to negative reviews more closely than the reviews themselves. Maintain absolute professional composure, even when you know the reviewer has never set foot in your store.

Step 1: Conduct an Immediate Internal Audit

Before you type a single word on your Google Business Profile dashboard, verify your facts. Check every internal touchpoint to ensure you did not genuinely miss a customer.

Look up their name across your CRM, POS billing system, appointment diaries, and communication channels. In India, where digital payments dominate, search your UPI transaction history (PhonePe, Google Pay, Paytm) for that name or phone number around the date mentioned. If you run a salon in Lower Parel or a car repair workshop in Anna Nagar, double-check your job cards and ledger entries.

If your audit conclusively shows zero records of transaction or interaction, you are dealing with an unverified or fraudulent review. Now it is time to act methodically.

Step 2: Write a Public Response That Protects Your Brand

Your public reply to a suspicious review is not meant for the scammer—it is meant for the hundreds of genuine prospective customers who will read it tomorrow. Your tone must be polite, factual, and resolute.

The golden rule is to publicly highlight the lack of records without sounding defensive. Here is a proven template you can adapt for your local business:

"Hello [Name]. We take all feedback very seriously at our establishment, but after thoroughly checking our appointment logs, customer database, and billing records for the date mentioned, we have no record of anyone by your name or description visiting us. We pride ourselves on transparent, top-quality service. Could you please reach out to our manager directly at [Phone/Email] with your bill number or exact visit details so we can investigate this immediately?" — Standard Professional Response Framework

This approach achieves three vital goals: it alerts casual readers that the review is likely fake, it shows you are proactive and customer-centric, and it challenges the reviewer to prove their claim.

Step 3: Report and Flag the Review to Google

Google’s automated moderation catches many fake reviews, but sophisticated spam slips through. You must formally report the review for violating Google's Terms of Service.

  • Log into your Google Business Profile dashboard on desktop or mobile.
  • Navigate to the "Reviews" tab and locate the specific fraudulent comment.
  • Click the three vertical dots next to the review and select "Report review".
  • Choose the most appropriate violation category, such as "Conflict of interest" (if it is a competitor) or "Spam".

If Google’s automated system rejects your initial flag, do not panic. You can escalate the issue via the Google Business Profile Help Community forum, where product experts can review policy violations manually.

Step 4: Build an Inimitable Shield of Verified Reviews

The best defense against occasional fake reviews is an overwhelming wall of authentic, verified customer feedback. When your local business holds a solid 4.8-star rating built on hundreds of recent reviews, a stray unverified 1-star comment loses all its power and looks like the obvious anomaly it is.

Proactive Measures for Total Reputation Safety

To bulletproof your local business against future review manipulation, implement these operational habits:

  • Digitise All Transactions: Maintain clean digital invoices linked to customer phone numbers. This gives you concrete evidence when disputing false claims with Google support.
  • Train Your Front Desk: Instruct receptionists and billing staff to encourage happy customers to leave feedback right at the counter using a quick QR code linked to your Google listing.
  • Monitor Weekly: Set up calendar reminders to review your Google Business Profile analytics and new submissions every Monday morning.
