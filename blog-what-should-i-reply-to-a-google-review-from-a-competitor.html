What Should I Reply to a Google Review From a Competitor?

RB
ReviewBoostPRO Editorial Team
• August 2026 • 7 min read

Picture this: You run a thriving dental clinic in Bandra, Mumbai, or perhaps a busy family restaurant in Koramangala, Bangalore. You open your Google Business Profile dashboard on a Tuesday morning, ready to check your latest patient or customer feedback. Instead of a cheerful 5-star rating, you spot a scathing 1-star review written by someone whose name sounds suspiciously familiar—or worse, a profile with zero context, no previous review history, and a generic complaint that never actually happened in your establishment.

Unfair, malicious, and fake reviews from local competitors are an unfortunate reality of doing business in India today. Whether it is a rival auto repair shop down the street in Delhi or a competing salon in Hyderabad trying to steal your foot traffic, dirty tricks do happen. But your reaction in the public comments section can make or break your local brand authority.

So, what exactly should you reply to a Google review from a competitor? Let’s break down the psychology, the right strategy, and the step-by-step templates to protect your business.

How to Spot a Competitor Review vs. A Real Customer Complaint

Before you draft a furious reply or accuse someone publicly, you need to be 100% certain it is a competitor. False accusations against a real client can backfire catastrophically. Look for these telltale signs:

  • Vague Details: Real customers describe specific items, staff names, or services (e.g., "Dr. Sharma was very gentle during my root canal"). Competitor reviews are usually blank or heavily generic (e.g., "Very bad service, arrogant staff, never visiting again").
  • Zero History or Suspicious Profiles: Click on the reviewer's profile. Often, they have left only one review in total—your business—or a cluster of 1-star reviews for several local businesses in the exact same niche.
  • Timing Coincidences: Did you recently run a successful promotional campaign, open a new branch, or outrank them on local Google Maps? Spikes in fake negative reviews often follow business wins.
Golden Rule #1 Never reply in anger. Remember: prospective customers in cities like Pune, Chennai, or Ahmedabad are reading your response to judge your professionalism, not just reading the fake review.

The Wrong Way to Reply (What to Avoid at All Costs)

When you spot a competitor trying to sabotage your ratings, your blood boils. It is tempting to write things like: "We know this is just [Competitor Name] trying to pull us down because our food is better!" or "Stop creating fake profiles, Rahul from the next lane!"

Avoid this trap. Here is why:

  • Google's Terms of Service: Publicly accusing someone of being a competitor without concrete proof can violate community guidelines or lead to messy defamation disputes.
  • Giving Them Oxygen: Naming a competitor in your reply draws direct attention to them, potentially driving curious prospective clients straight to their doorstep.
  • Unprofessional Image: Future customers scrolling through your profile see a defensive business owner rather than a calm, customer-first establishment.

Exact Response Templates for Competitor Reviews

Your public reply should serve two masters: Google's automated moderation algorithms (which look for polite, professional dispute resolution text) and prospective customers who are evaluating your trustworthiness.

Here are three professional templates you can adapt:

Template 1: The Polite Fact-Checker (Best when the event never happened)
"Hello [Name], we take all feedback seriously, but our records show no record of you visiting our [clinic/restaurant/salon] on this date or using our services. We pride ourselves on top-tier quality for all our patrons in [City Name]. If you are a genuine client, please reach out to our manager directly at [Phone Number/Email] with details so we can investigate immediately."

Template 2: The Direct & Concise Deflection (Best for obviously empty profiles)
"Hello. We have checked our client logs and CCTV records for the mentioned date and could not find any transaction or visit matching your profile. If you had an actual experience with us, kindly contact us directly with your invoice details so we can resolve it."

Step 2: How to Actually Remove the Review Through Google

Replying is only the first line of defense. You must also report the review to Google for policy violations (such as spam, harassment, or conflict of interest).

  1. Log into your Google Business Profile dashboard.
  2. Navigate to the Reviews tab and locate the suspicious review.
  3. Click the three vertical dots next to the review and select "Report review" or "Report as inappropriate".
  4. Choose the most accurate violation reason (e.g., "Conflict of interest" or "Spam").

If Google does not remove it automatically within a few days, you can escalate the ticket via the Google Business Profile Help Community forum where product experts review flagged edge cases.
