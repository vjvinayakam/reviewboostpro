What to Do If a Competitor Leaves a Negative Google Review

RB
ReviewBoostPRO Editorial Team
• August 14, 2026 • 7 min read

Imagine waking up on a busy Tuesday morning in Mumbai, opening your Google Business Profile, and noticing that your 4.8-star rating has suddenly dropped because of a blistering 1-star review. You read through the complaint—supposedly about poor service or rude staff—but nothing matches your records. You check your billing software, ask your team, and realize the awful truth: none of your actual customers experienced this. It’s a fake, malicious review planted by a local competitor.

Unfortunately, in the hyper-competitive local business ecosystems across Indian cities like Delhi, Bangalore, Chennai, Pune, and Hyderabad, dirty tricks happen every day. Rival dental clinics, neighborhood pharmacies, auto repair garages, popular cafes, and high-street salons occasionally resort to unfair digital sabotage to siphon away foot traffic.

Discovering a competitor's sabotage attempt can make your blood boil. However, reacting out of anger can easily backfire. In this comprehensive guide, we will walk you through exactly how to spot a competitor-driven negative review, how to respond professionally, how to report it to Google, and how to bulletproof your business against future attacks.

1. How to Confirm the Review is From a Competitor

Before you accuse anyone or flag a review, you need certainty. You cannot simply label every critical review as "fake" just because it hurts your feelings. Look for these distinct red flags that typically expose competitor interference:

  • Vague or Generic Complaints: The review uses sweeping statements like "Very bad service" or "Worst place in town" without naming specific products, treatments, menu items, or employee names.
  • The "Ghost Customer" Profile: Click on the reviewer's profile picture or name. Often, they have zero other reviews, or worse, their review history consists entirely of 5-star ratings for one specific business—your direct competitor down the street.
  • Timing Anomalies: The review drops during off-hours, late at night, or right before a major festival weekend when you are running high-impact promotions.
  • Incorrect Terminology: A review left for an auto repair shop complaining about "poor medicine quality," or a dental clinic review complaining about "cold coffee." Cross-industry confusion is a dead giveaway of copy-pasted fake reviews.
Important Reality Check Never publicly accuse the competitor in your response. Even if you know for 100% certainty that Dr. Sharma from the clinic next door paid someone to write it, publicly calling them out makes you look defensive and unprofessional to prospective customers browsing your profile.

2. Crafting the Perfect Public Response

Even though the review is fake, your response is not for the saboteur—it is for the hundreds of genuine local customers who will read it in the future. Your tone must remain calm, empathetic, and objective.

Your goal is to highlight to readers that this review doesn't match your service standards while inviting them to resolve the issue offline. Here is a proven template you can adapt for your Indian business:

"Hello [Name], we take our service quality extremely seriously at our [clinic/restaurant/salon] in Bangalore, but we have no record of any customer with your name or transaction details visiting us on this date. We would love to investigate this immediately to verify if there was any genuine lapse. Please reach out to our manager directly at [Phone Number/Email] so we can sort this out." — Standard Professional Response Template

When prospective clients read this, they immediately realize two things: first, that you care deeply about customer satisfaction, and second, that the reviewer’s identity or visit is questionable.

3. Step-by-Step Guide to Reporting Fake Reviews to Google

Google has strict guidelines against conflict of interest and fraudulent reviews. You have the right to flag and appeal reviews that violate Google’s Terms of Service. Follow these steps:

  • Log into your Google Business Profile Manager dashboard.
  • Navigate to the Reviews tab and locate the suspicious review.
  • Click on the three vertical dots next to the review and select "Report review" or "Flag as inappropriate".
  • Choose the most accurate violation category—usually "Conflict of interest" (if it's a competitor) or "Spam".
  • If Google’s automated system rejects your initial flag, do not panic. Head over to the Google Business Profile Help Community, create a thread, provide screenshots, and explain your case clearly to a Product Expert.

The absolute best antidote to a competitor's negative review is an overwhelming flood of positive reviews from your actual, happy customers. If you have a 4.9-star rating built on 800 authentic reviews, one solitary 1-star review from a saboteur looks like an obvious outlier that no reasonable person will take seriously.

5. Legal and Ethical Boundaries: When to Escalate

In extreme cases where a competitor launches a coordinated defamation campaign—leaving dozens of fake reviews, posting defamatory photos, or harassing your staff online—you have legal recourse under Indian law.

Under the Information Technology Act and the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita (BNS), deliberate online defamation and digital sabotage carry severe penalties. You can file a formal complaint with the Cyber Cell of your city (such as the Cyber Crime divisions in Delhi, Chennai, or Bangalore) and send a formal legal cease-and-desist notice through an advocate. Usually, the threat of legal action is enough to make cowardly competitors delete their fake profiles overnight.
