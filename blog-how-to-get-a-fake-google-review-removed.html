How to Get a Fake Google Review Removed (Step-by-Step Guide for Indian Businesses)

ReviewBoostPRO Team

Picture this: You open your Google Business Profile dashboard on a busy Monday morning in Mumbai or Delhi, expecting to see updates from your clinic, restaurant, or salon. Instead, you notice a fresh 1-star rating with no text, or worse, a malicious paragraph filled with absolute falsehoods claiming your staff behaved inappropriately or your service was substandard. You check your appointment records or customer logs—no such person exists. Panic sets in. That single fake review threatens to drag down your rating and cost you thousands of rupees in lost local footfall.

Fake reviews are an unfortunate reality for local businesses across India. Whether it is an aggressive local competitor trying to siphon away your patients in Bangalore, a disgruntled ex-employee in Chennai, or random internet trolls, malicious reviews can severely damage your local reputation. Fortunately, Google has a formal process for disputing and removing reviews that violate its content policies. In this comprehensive guide, we will break down exactly how you can identify, report, and successfully remove fake Google reviews to protect your business.

Step 1: Verify That the Review Actually Violates Google’s Policies

Before you hit the report button, you must understand that Google will not take down a review simply because you disagree with it or because a customer was unhappy. Google protects consumer free speech, meaning genuine negative feedback—even if harsh or unfair—usually stays up.

To get a review removed, it must violate Google’s prohibited and restricted content guidelines. Look closely for the following violations:

  • Spam and Fake Content: The reviewer has posted identical reviews across multiple competing businesses, or the account appears to be a bot or fake profile with zero connection to your establishment.
  • Conflict of Interest: The reviewer is a direct competitor, a current or former disgruntled employee trying to sabotage your business, or someone incentivized to manipulate your rating.
  • Harassment or Hate Speech: The review contains personal attacks against your staff, abusive language, or discriminatory remarks.
  • Irrelevant or Off-Topic Content: The text talks about a completely different business, a political rant, or general grievances unrelated to the actual service provided at your shop or clinic.
Important Warning Do not engage in online arguments or use abusive language when replying to a fake review. Your public reply should remain calm, professional, and state clearly that you have no record of their visit. Future customers will read this and realize the review is likely fraudulent.

Step 2: How to Flag the Review Through Your Google Business Profile

Once you are certain the review violates Google's policies, you can flag it directly from your dashboard or Google Maps. Here is the exact step-by-step process:

  • Log in to your Google Business Profile account using the Google account managed for your business.
  • Navigate to your business listing and select "Reviews" from the menu options.
  • Scroll down to find the suspicious review, click on the three vertical dots (menu icon) next to the review, and select "Report review".
  • You will be redirected to Google’s Support page where you must select the specific reason why the review violates policies (e.g., "Conflict of interest", "Spam", or "Off-topic").
  • Submit your report. Google will generate a case ID for your tracking purposes.

Step 3: Track and Escalate via the Google Business Profile Help Community

Flagging a review is automatic, and frankly, automated filters don't catch everything. If your initial flag is rejected or ignored by the system, don't lose hope. There is a secondary escalation method available to Indian business owners.

You can take your case to the official Google Business Profile Help Community forum. Create a new post detailing your situation clearly. Provide screenshots of the review, evidence showing why it is fake (such as lack of bills, missing customer records, or blatant competitor bias), and mention your original case ID. Product Experts (trusted community volunteers designated by Google) review these forum posts and can escalate genuine violations directly to Google’s internal engineering teams for manual review.
