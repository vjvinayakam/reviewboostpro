Can One Customer Leave Multiple Google Reviews for Your Business?

RB
ReviewBoostPRO Editorial Team
• August 2026 • 7 min read

Imagine this scenario: A loyal patron visits your restaurant in Bandra, Mumbai, every single weekend. Or perhaps a patient visits a multi-specialty dental clinic in Koramangala, Bangalore, for monthly root canal follow-ups. They love your service, they appreciate your staff, and they want to help your local business rank higher on Google Maps. Naturally, they ask: "Can I leave another review for you today?"

It is a completely natural thought process for a delighted customer. However, when it comes to Google Business Profile (GBP) optimization and local SEO guidelines, the mechanics of customer reviews are strictly governed. Business owners across India—from bustling auto repair workshops in Pune to boutique salons in Delhi—often wonder how Google handles repeat feedback from the same individual.

Let's dive deep into Google's policies, what happens when a customer tries to post a second review, and how you can ethically maximize your review velocity without violating terms of service.

The Short Answer: No, One Google Account = One Active Review

To put it simply: No, a single customer cannot leave multiple active reviews on your Google Business Profile using the same Google account.

Google’s core philosophy for reviews is built on authenticity, transparency, and consumer trust. Allowing a single individual to flood a business profile with dozens of reviews would easily skew local search rankings, making it vulnerable to manipulation. Therefore, Google enforces a strict limit: one review per Google account per business location.

If a customer who has already reviewed your pharmacy in Chennai attempts to leave a brand-new review from the same Google profile months later, Google’s system handles it in one of two ways:

  • The Update Mechanism: Instead of creating a second review, Google will prompt the user to update their existing review. Their new star rating and text will overwrite their previous submission. Your total review count remains exactly the same, though the date and content of the review might change.
  • Algorithmic Filtering: If the system detects suspicious patterns of duplicate submissions or artificial inflation from specific accounts, the additional review attempts are typically filtered out entirely and hidden from public view.
Important Note for Business Owners Never encourage customers to create secondary Google accounts just to leave you more reviews. Google's sophisticated spam filters track IP addresses, device signatures, and behavioral patterns. Engaging in review manipulation can lead to your Google Business Profile being suspended overnight.

What Happens When a Customer Updates an Existing Review?

While a customer cannot add a second review, updating an existing review is entirely permissible and quite common. For instance, a customer might have left a 4-star review for your hotel in Hyderabad six months ago. After returning for another stay and experiencing upgraded rooms, they decide to change it to a 5-star rating and add fresh compliments.

When a customer updates their review:

  • The overall review count on your listing does not increase (since they already had one review counted).
  • Your average star rating might shift upward or downward depending on the new rating.
  • The timestamp on the review updates to reflect the latest modification, signaling to prospective customers that your business maintains consistent quality over time.

If your local business thrives on repeat visits—such as a fitness studio in Connaught Place, Delhi, or an upscale salon in Jubilee Hills, Hyderabad—you naturally have many customers who interact with you dozens of times a year. How do you harness their ongoing goodwill without breaking Google's rules?

Instead of asking the same person to leave multiple reviews on Google, channel their ongoing satisfaction into other high-impact growth strategies:

  • Ask for Word-of-Mouth Referrals: Encourage your happiest repeat customers to share your business card or WhatsApp link with their friends, family, and colleagues.

  • Focus on Fresh Review Acquisition: Rather than relying on old customers to review you twice, focus your energy on systematically capturing reviews from your new daily footfall.

Multi-Location Businesses: A Special Exception

There is one unique scenario where a single customer can leave multiple reviews for the same brand: multi-location businesses.

If your enterprise operates separate branches—for example, a diagnostic chain with locations in Andheri, Dadar, and Thane in Mumbai—a customer who visits all three branches can legitimately leave a separate Google review for each distinct business location. Each location operates on its own independent Google Business Profile listing with a unique Place ID.
