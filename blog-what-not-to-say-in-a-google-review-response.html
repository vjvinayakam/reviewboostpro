What Not to Say in a Google Review Response (Mistakes That Can Cost You Customers)

RB
ReviewBoostPRO Team
• August 2026 • 7 min read

Managing a local business in India is no small feat. Whether you run a bustling dental clinic in Mumbai, a popular family restaurant in Bangalore, a premium salon in Delhi, or an auto repair shop in Pune, your online reputation can make or break your daily walk-ins. When a happy customer leaves a 5-star review, or an unhappy client vents their frustration with a 1-star rating, your response is public for thousands of prospective customers to see.

Unfortunately, many business owners reply in the heat of the moment, making critical errors that damage their brand image far more than the original negative review ever could. In the competitive Indian marketplace, prospective clients read your replies to understand your professionalism, customer service ethics, and accountability.

Let's examine the most damaging things you should never say in a Google review response, and how you can handle both positive and negative feedback with absolute poise.

1. "We have no record of you ever visiting our shop."

This is one of the most common knee-jerk reactions business owners have to a negative review. A clinic owner in Hyderabad or a pharmacy owner in Chennai might read a harsh complaint and immediately write: "We check all our bills and patient logs, and you were never a customer here. This is fake!"

Even if you strongly suspect the review is spam or a malicious attack from a competitor, telling a reviewer they were never there sounds defensive, petty, and dismissive to outside readers. To anyone browsing your Google Business Profile, it looks like you are trying to hide genuine criticism.

Instead, try this: "We take our service quality extremely seriously, but we are unable to match your name or details to any recent visits in our system. Could you please reach out to our manager directly at [Phone Number] or drop us a message so we can look into this immediately?"

2. Getting Emotional or Defensive

When someone attacks your livelihood, passion, or hard work, it is entirely natural to feel angry. However, public review sections are not the place to settle scores. Writing sentences like "You were rude to our staff first" or "Stop spreading lies about our restaurant, you ruined your own evening" transforms a customer service issue into a public spectacle.

Prospective customers rarely side with a business owner who loses their temper online. No matter how unreasonable the reviewer is being, your response must remain remarkably calm, polite, and objective.

3. Violating Privacy Laws or Sharing Personal Details

In your haste to defend your business, you might be tempted to bring up specific details of the interaction to prove your point—such as medical conditions, exact billing amounts in ₹, or personal conversations. Doing this is not only unethical; it can also land you in serious legal trouble regarding customer privacy and data protection.

Never disclose private information, medical histories, or financial transaction details in a public Google review response. Keep the conversation high-level and transition the details to a private communication channel.

4. Offering Generic, Robotic Copy-Paste Replies to 5-Star Reviews

While negative reviews require careful damage control, positive reviews are your goldmine for local SEO and social proof. Yet, many business owners make the mistake of copy-pasting the exact same response—such as "Thanks for the review!"—to every single customer.

When local consumers see hundreds of identical, robotic replies, it signals that the business doesn't genuinely care about its patrons. Personalized responses that mention the customer's name, the specific service they received, or a warm local touch show authentic appreciation.

5. Arguing About Prices or Policies Publicly

If a customer leaves a review complaining that your salon services in Kolkata are too expensive or that your hotel refund policy is unfair, arguing with them in public is a losing battle. Stating things like "Our prices are clearly listed on the wall" or "Everyone else pays this fee without complaining" makes your business look inflexible and uncaring.

Policies can be explained calmly, but a public forum is never the right place to debate commercial terms. Acknowledge their perspective and invite them to discuss the matter privately.

6. Ignoring Negative Reviews Entirely

While this is an omission rather than a statement, ignoring negative reviews sends a loud message. If prospective clients see unresolved 1-star ratings from months ago with zero acknowledgment from the business, they will assume you simply do not care about customer satisfaction.

Addressing a negative review professionally proves to future readers that even when things go wrong, your management team steps up to make things right.

Summary Checklist: Golden Rules for Review Responses

  • Acknowledge and Validate: Always thank the reviewer for taking the time to share their feedback, positive or negative.
  • Take It Offline: Provide a direct phone number or email address to resolve complex grievances away from the public eye.
  • Stay Professional: Maintain a courteous, calm tone that reflects your brand's core values.
  • Leverage Automation: Streamline your positive review collection through trusted systems like ReviewBoostPRO so you drown out stray complaints with an overwhelming volume of genuine 5-star ratings.
