What Should I Reply to a Customer Who Says We Didn't Listen?

RB
ReviewBoostPRO Editorial Team
• August 2026 • 7 min read

Picture this: It is a bustling Saturday evening at your popular family restaurant in Bandra, Mumbai, or your busy dermatology clinic in Koramangala, Bangalore. Amidst the rush of managing patients, tables, and staff, a customer walks out unhappy. A day later, a stinging 1-star Google review pops up on your listing. The core of their grievance? "The staff simply refused to listen to what I wanted."

For Indian business owners, hearing that you "didn't listen" hits a raw nerve. We take immense pride in our hospitality, patient care, and customer relationships ("Atithi Devo Bhava"). When someone accuses your business of ignoring them publicly, your first instinct is likely defensive. You want to type out all the reasons why your clinic was rushed, or why the auto repair shop mechanic was working under extreme pressure.

Stop. Take a deep breath. Responding defensively to a customer who feels unheard will only fuel the fire and alienate prospective customers reading your reviews.

Why the "Not Listening" Complaint is Uniquely Dangerous

When an Indian consumer complains about slow service or high pricing, it is transactional. They felt they didn’t get value for their ₹500 or ₹2,000. But when a customer writes that you did not listen, it feels personal. It strikes at the dignity and emotional core of the interaction.

In local business verticals—whether you run a dental clinic in Chennai, a boutique hair salon in Greater Kailash, Delhi, or a pharmacy in Pune—listening is the foundation of trust. If a patient feels their symptoms were dismissed, or a salon client feels the stylist chopped off three inches instead of the requested half-inch because nobody listened, the damage goes deeper than a lost transaction. It signals incompetence and apathy.

However, handled correctly, a review complaining about poor listening can actually become your greatest trust-builder. Prospective customers scanning your Google profile don't expect perfection; they look at how you handle imperfection.

The Danger Zone Never reply with: "We were very busy that day so we couldn't give you time," or "Our staff followed standard protocol, you misunderstood." This validates their accusation that you are still not listening to them!

The 4-Step Framework for Responding to "Not Listening" Complaints

When drafting your public reply on Google Business Profile, your goal is twofold: validate the customer's emotional frustration without necessarily admitting fault to every detail, and pivot the conversation offline.

  • Step 1: Acknowledge and Validate Immediately. Use the customer's name (e.g., "Dear Rajesh Ji"). Acknowledge that they left feeling unheard. Validating does not mean you accept blame; it means you respect their emotional experience.
  • Step 2: Remove the Defensive Barrier. Avoid excuses about heavy footfall, festival rushes, or short-staffing. Even if true, a customer does not care about your operational stress when they are paying for personalized attention.
  • Step 3: State Your Core Value. Remind the reader (and the reviewer) that listening to customers is your utmost priority, making this incident an anomaly.
  • Step 4: Take it Offline with a Direct Contact. Provide a direct phone number or name of the owner/manager so they can resolve the issue personally.

Ready-to-Use Reply Templates for Different Industries

To make this practical, let's look at how local businesses across India can adapt these principles in real-world scenarios.

1. For Healthcare Clinics & Pharmacies (e.g., Delhi / Hyderabad)

"Dear Amit Ji, thank you for sharing your feedback. We are genuinely sorry that you felt rushed and that our team didn't listen carefully to your health concerns during your visit yesterday. Patient care and active listening are pillars of our practice, and it is clear we fell short of our own standards in your case. Please reach out to me directly at 98200-XXXXX so I can personally understand your symptoms and ensure you receive the proper attention from our senior consultant. Warm regards, Dr. Sharma."

2. For Salons & Spas (e.g., Bangalore / Pune)

"Hi Priya, we are deeply concerned to read this. The last thing we want is for any client to leave our salon feeling like their styling preferences were ignored. We always strive to get the details right. I would love to invite you back for a complimentary consultation and corrective session with our master stylist. Please drop me a direct message or call our salon manager at 98450-XXXXX. Thank you for holding us accountable.—Management, Glow & Style Studio."

3. For Restaurants & Cafes (e.g., Mumbai / Chennai)

"Dear Vikram, thank you for bringing this to our attention. We pride ourselves on getting dietary preferences and table requests right every single time, and we sincerely apologize that our team failed to listen to your instructions regarding your order. We want to make this right. Please call our general manager at 98840-XXXXX so we can invite you back and treat you to the experience you truly deserve."
