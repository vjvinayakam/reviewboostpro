How to Respond to a Review You Believe Is Fake Without Accusing the Reviewer

RB
ReviewBoostPRO Team
• August 2026 • 7 min read

Imagine running a bustling dental clinic in South Mumbai or a popular multi-cuisine restaurant in Koramangala, Bangalore. You pride yourself on your service, your staff works hard to keep customers smiling, and your local reputation is your most prized asset. Then, out of nowhere, a 1-star review pops up on your Google Business Profile. The comment is completely vague: "Worst experience ever. Terrible service." There's no purchase detail, no staff name mentioned, and when you check your patient or customer logs for the day, that name doesn't exist anywhere in your system.

Your blood pressure spikes. Your immediate instinct is to reply furiously: "You are a liar! We have never seen you at our clinic, this is clearly a fake review by a competitor!"

Stop right there. While your anger is entirely justified, hitting "Post" on an angry, accusatory response is the single worst thing you can do for your business.

Why You Must Never Accuse a Reviewer Publicly

Google reviews are not private dispute channels; they are a public billboard for your business. Prospective customers across India—whether they are searching for an auto repair shop in Pune or a pharmacy in Delhi—are reading how you handle adversity. If you respond aggressively, accuse someone of lying, or sound defensive, future customers won't see a victimized business owner. Instead, they will see a hostile, unprofessional merchant who cannot handle criticism.

Furthermore, Google’s automated content moderation guidelines and support teams rarely side with business owners who throw accusations. If you flag a review for removal, Google looks at your public response. If your reply is combative, it diminishes your credibility during the manual review appeal process.

The Golden Rule of Online Reputation Never write your public response for the reviewer. Always write your response for the hundreds of prospective customers who will read it next week before deciding to visit your establishment.

The Psychology of Writing a "Neutral-Yet-Firm" Response

When dealing with a suspicious or fraudulent review, your objective is twofold: signal to casual readers that this review lacks credibility, while maintaining absolute professional courtesy. You want to highlight the absence of factual information without ever using words like "fake," "liar," or "competitor."

To achieve this, shift your tone from emotional to inquisitive and administrative. Frame your response around your inability to verify their transaction in your records. This exposes the review as baseless without sounding defensive.

Step-by-Step Framework for Crafting Your Response

Let's break down the exact anatomy of a professional response to a suspected fake review:

  • Acknowledge and Professional Greeting: Start politely. Address the user by their display name (e.g., "Hello Rahul,").
  • State the Discrepancy Politely: Clearly state that your team has searched your customer or patient records and could not locate any transaction or visit matching their account.
  • Invite Direct Offline Communication: Provide a direct phone number or email address so they can substantiate their claim, shifting the burden of proof onto them.
  • Keep It Concise: Do not write a 500-word essay. Short, calm, and collected responses project utmost confidence.

Real-World Templates You Can Copy and Adapt

Here are three ready-to-use templates tailored for different types of local businesses in India:

Template 1: For Clinics & Healthcare Providers
"Hello [Name], patient care and record accuracy are our utmost priorities at our clinic. However, after carefully checking our appointment registers and patient database for the dates mentioned, we are unable to find any record of a consultation under your name or details. We take all feedback seriously—could you please reach out directly to our clinic manager at [Phone Number] or [Email] so we can identify your file and assist you immediately?"

Template 2: For Restaurants & Cafes
"Hi [Name], we pride ourselves on delivering great food and hospitality. We've reviewed our tables, bills, and reservation logs for the past week, but we can't find any record of your visit to our establishment. If you did dine with us, we would love to know what went wrong. Please connect with our restaurant manager at [Phone Number] with your bill details so we can investigate this right away."

Template 3: For Salons, Spas & Retail Stores
"Hello [Name], thank you for sharing your feedback. We maintain thorough appointment and billing records for all our clients, but we cannot locate any transaction associated with your account in our system. We would love to clear up any misunderstanding. Please call our store manager directly at [Phone Number] so we can look into this matter further."

Pro Tip from Industry Experts When you politely state that they cannot be found in your records, genuine customers with a real grievance will usually reply with receipt numbers, appointment times, or clarification. Fake reviewers or malicious competitors, on the other hand, almost never reply because they have nothing to back up their claims.

Taking the Next Step: Flagging and Reporting to Google

Once you have posted your calm, professional response, your work isn't done. You must formally report the review to Google for violating their terms of service (specifically regarding spam or fake engagement).

Log into your Google Business Profile dashboard, locate the review, click the three-dot menu next to it, and select "Report review." Choose the category that best fits—usually "Spam" or "Conflict of interest" if you suspect a local rival.
