SMS vs Email vs WhatsApp for Google Review Requests in India

RB
ReviewBoostPRO Team
• August 2026 • 9 min read

If you run a local business in India—whether it is a busy dental clinic in Bandra, Mumbai, a popular multi-cuisine restaurant in Koramangala, Bangalore, or an auto repair shop in Pune—you already know how important Google reviews are. When a prospective customer searches for "best dermatologist near me" or "top-rated salon in Delhi", your average rating and total review count can make or break their decision to walk through your doors.

However, getting customers to actually leave a review after they complete a transaction has always been a monumental challenge. For years, business owners relied on traditional communication channels like Email and SMS. But in today's fast-paced digital Indian economy, consumer behavior has shifted dramatically. Let’s break down the ultimate battleground of communication channels: SMS vs. Email vs. WhatsApp for gathering Google reviews.

The State of Email for Review Requests in India

Email was once the undisputed king of digital marketing. For B2B businesses or large SaaS companies, email works wonderfully. But for local brick-and-mortar businesses in India, email marketing for review generation is practically dead on arrival.

Consider the average Indian consumer. They open their personal email inbox maybe once a week—often just to check bank statements, utility bills from CESC or Tata Power, or flight tickets. Promotional emails, newsletters, and post-purchase feedback requests get swallowed up by the dreaded Promotions tab or outright spam folders.

  • Abysmally low open rates: Average open rates for post-purchase review emails in India hover around a dismal 12% to 15%.
  • Friction-heavy process: Even if a customer opens the email, they are usually asked to click a link, log into a browser, sometimes log into their Google account, and then leave feedback. Too many steps mean zero action.
  • Zero personal touch: Automated email templates feel cold, robotic, and easily ignorable.
The Verdict on Email Skip email if you want rapid review growth for your local storefront. By the time an Indian consumer checks their inbox, your service or meal is a distant memory.

The Limitations of SMS for Indian Local Businesses

SMS initially seemed like a massive upgrade over email. It lands directly on the user's phone, and notification pings are hard to ignore. Many businesses in Chennai and Hyderabad still use bulk SMS gateways to blast customers with review links.

However, SMS comes with severe bottlenecks in the Indian market:

  • TRAI DLT Regulations: Strict regulatory frameworks by the Telecom Regulatory Authority of India (TRAI) make sending transactional and promotional SMS an administrative nightmare. Getting template approvals takes days, and number blacklisting is common.
  • The Spam Epidemic: Your average Indian smartphone user receives dozens of spam SMS messages every single day—promoting loan offers, real estate in Greater Noida, and stock tips. Your genuine review request gets lumped in with spam and deleted unread.
  • Plain text limitations: Traditional SMS lacks rich media. You cannot send clickable buttons, engaging emojis, or friendly images that guide the customer seamlessly to your Google Business Profile.

Why WhatsApp is the Undisputed Winner in India

India is effectively a WhatsApp-first nation. From ordering groceries via quick-commerce to chatting with family, booking cab rides, and receiving medical reports from diagnostic centers in Hyderabad, WhatsApp is woven into the daily fabric of over 500 million Indians.

When you use WhatsApp to request Google reviews, you meet your customers where they are already spending hours every single day. The numbers speak for themselves:

  • Staggering Open Rates: WhatsApp messages boast an unprecedented 98% open rate, with the vast majority read within 3 minutes of delivery.
  • Instant Engagement: Unlike email, a WhatsApp message creates an immediate conversational prompt. It feels personal, warm, and attentive.
  • Frictionless Tap-to-Review: With verified WhatsApp business integrations, you can include direct, one-click links that take the customer straight to your Google review submission screen. No extra passwords or convoluted logins required.

Direct Comparison: SMS vs. Email vs. WhatsApp

To put things into crystal clear perspective, let's look at how these three communication channels stack up across critical metrics for local Indian businesses:

  • Open Rate: Email (12-15%) | SMS (45-60%) | WhatsApp (95-98%)
  • Response Time: Email (24-48 hours) | SMS (1-3 hours) | WhatsApp (Under 5 minutes)
  • Regulatory Friction: Email (Low) | SMS (Very High via TRAI/DLT) | WhatsApp (Moderate via Official API)
  • Customer Preference: Email (Low) | SMS (Neutral) | WhatsApp (Extremely High)
  • Review Conversion Rate: Email (<1%) | SMS (2-4%) | WhatsApp (18-25%)

How to Implement a Winning WhatsApp Review Strategy

Switching to WhatsApp is half the battle. To maximize your results, timing and personalization are everything. Here is a proven blueprint used by top-performing retail stores in Mumbai and pharmacies in Kolkata:

  • Automate the trigger: Do not rely on manual staff reminders. Integrate your billing software or point-of-sale system so that the moment a receipt is generated, a WhatsApp sequence kicks off.
  • Keep the message concise and warm: Address the customer by their first name. Express genuine gratitude for their visit to your establishment.
  • Use a direct link: Make sure the call-to-action button or link leads directly to your Google Maps review dialog box, bypassing unnecessary landing pages.
Pro Tip for Local Businesses Timing is everything. Send your WhatsApp review request within 30 to 60 minutes after the service is rendered, while your staff's hospitality or your product's quality is fresh in the customer's mind.

Managing this manually across hundreds of daily customers is impossible. That is where automated tools bridge the gap, ensuring zero manual follow-ups while protecting your brand reputation.

Conclusion

The data leaves no room for debate. While email and SMS had their golden eras, they are rapidly losing effectiveness in modern India. Consumers demand speed, convenience, and personal connection.
