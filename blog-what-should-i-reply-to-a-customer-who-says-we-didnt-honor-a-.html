What Should I Reply to a Customer Who Says We Didn't Honor a Refund?

RB
Editorial Team
• August 14, 2026 • 7 min read

Picture this: It is a busy Saturday evening at your establishment—whether it is a bustling family restaurant in Bandra, Mumbai, a popular dental clinic in Koramangala, Bangalore, or a premium salon in Greater Kailash, Delhi. You open your Google Business Profile dashboard to check your visibility, and you see a stinging 1-star review.

The review reads: "Worst experience! They promised a refund of ₹2,500 after a canceled service, but it has been two weeks and they completely cheated me. They do not honor their refunds."

Your heart sinks. As a local business owner in India, your reputation is everything. Word of mouth travels fast, and a public accusation of financial dishonesty can instantly deter potential customers searching for trusted services online. But before you react out of anger or frustration, pause. How you respond publicly to a refund complaint can either salvage your brand's integrity or permanently damage it.

Why Public Refund Complaints Are Uniquely Dangerous

When a customer posts a review claiming you stole their money or refused to honor a financial commitment, prospective clients view it through a lens of high skepticism. Unlike a complaint about cold food or a delayed appointment, money issues touch a nerve of trust. If left unaddressed or handled poorly, it signals to future patrons that your business lacks basic ethics.

Moreover, Google's algorithm rewards businesses that maintain active, professional, and empathetic engagement with their reviewers. Ignoring the comment or responding defensively ("We never promised this, you are lying!") will only escalate the conflict and validate the customer's negative claims in the eyes of casual readers.

The Golden Rule of Public Replies Never argue transactional details, bank transaction IDs, or internal store policies in a public review comment. Your public reply is not meant to win an argument with the reviewer; it is meant to demonstrate professionalism to the hundreds of prospective customers who will read it tomorrow.

Step 1: De-escalate and Validate Emotion (Without Admitting Fault)

The very first sentence of your response must diffuse tension. Even if you believe the customer is mistaken or that their refund is already processing through banking channels, start by acknowledging their frustration. Acknowledge that waiting for money is stressful.

Example opening: "Hello Rahul, we completely understand your frustration regarding the delay in your refund of ₹2,500, and we apologize for any anxiety this situation has caused you."

This approach shows observers that your business is empathetic and polite, rather than combative or dismissive.

Step 2: Take the Conversation Offline Immediately

Never hash out bank account numbers, UPI references, or receipt numbers on a public Google forum. It compromises customer privacy and opens the door to messy, drawn-out public debates.

Direct the customer to a private communication channel where your team can resolve the financial matter swiftly. In India, offering a direct WhatsApp contact or a dedicated manager phone number works wonders because customers appreciate instant, personal communication.

Example pivot: "We want to resolve this immediately. Could you please drop a quick message to our manager, Amit, directly on our business WhatsApp at +91 98200 XXXXX with your registered mobile number and transaction receipt? We will verify the bank status and clear this up today."

Step 3: Investigate Behind the Scenes

Once the customer moves the conversation to WhatsApp or phone calls, investigate what actually went wrong. In the Indian digital payments ecosystem, refund discrepancies usually stem from one of three common issues:

  • UPI / Gateway Delays: Razorpay, Paytm, or PhonePe refund settlements can occasionally take 5 to 7 working days to reflect in the customer's bank account, even if your POS machine or payment gateway dashboard shows "Refund Initiated."
  • Data Entry Errors: A staff member might have noted the wrong UPI handle or bank account details during the initial refund request.
  • Miscommunication on Store Policy: The customer might have expected an instant cash refund for a digital card transaction, which legally and procedurally must route back to the original source card.

Step 4: Swift Resolution and the Power Follow-Up

Once you locate the bottleneck—whether it is pushing the transaction manually through your merchant panel or explaining the banking clearing cycle—fix it urgently. If it was your business's administrative delay, apologize sincerely and expedite the transfer.

Once the refund successfully hits the customer's account and they confirm receipt, this is your golden opportunity. Do not let the interaction end there. Strike while the iron is hot and the customer is feeling relieved and appreciative.

Many local business owners miss this step because they are relieved the complaint is gone. However, satisfied customers who previously left negative reviews are often willing to update or delete their feedback once you make things right.
