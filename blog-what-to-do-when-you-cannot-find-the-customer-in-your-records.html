What to Do When You Cannot Find the Customer in Your Records

RB
By ReviewBoostPRO Team
• August 14, 2026 • 7 min read

Imagine this scenario: You sit down with your morning filter coffee in your Bangalore clinic or check your dashboard at your busy Pune auto repair shop. A new 5-star Google review has popped up overnight. You smile, but then you look closer at the name. "Amit K." or "Priya Sharma." You flip through your appointment registry, check your digital billing software, and cross-reference your recent UPI transaction history. Nothing. Absolutely zero trace of this person.

For local business owners across India—from Mumbai dental practices and Chennai pharmacies to Delhi salons and Hyderabad hotels—this is a surprisingly common dilemma. When a review arrives from a ghost customer, you are left staring at a puzzle. Is it a genuine patron who used a different name online? Is it a competitor playing dirty? Or is it an overly enthusiastic friend of an employee?

Handling unverified reviews incorrectly can either alienate a genuine, paying customer or leave your business vulnerable to fake feedback manipulation. In this guide, we explore why this happens and outline a step-by-step framework to handle unidentified reviewers professionally.

Why Does This Happen So Often in Indian Businesses?

Before panicking, it helps to understand why your customer database and your Google Maps notification are not matching up. The digital naming conventions in India create massive discrepancies between real-world identities and online profiles.

First, privacy and anonymity drive many users to adopt abbreviations, initials, or pseudonyms on their Google accounts. A customer named Venkatachalam Ramanathan might save his Google profile as simply "V. Raman." When he visits your Chennai electronics store and pays via Google Pay or PhonePe, his invoice carries his formal banking name, completely blinding your front desk.

Second, family dynamics play a huge role in local commerce. A husband might bring his car for servicing at your workshop, but the Google review is written from the wife's smartphone using her account because she handles the family's digital footprint. Similarly, in multi-doctor clinics in Mumbai or busy family restaurants in Delhi, one member books the appointment, while another leaves the feedback.

The Danger of Instant Reaction Never immediately flag or publicly accuse a reviewer of being fake just because their name is missing from your immediate billing logs. A public defensive outburst can destroy the goodwill you have spent years building in your local community.

Step 1: Dig Deeper Than Your Primary Registry

When an unfamiliar name drops a review, don't stop at your main appointment book. Cast a wider net across your operational touchpoints to locate their transaction:

  • Check Digital Wallets: Search your UPI merchant apps (Paytm, PhonePe, Google Pay Business) for partial name matches or the exact time stamp of your operating hours.
  • Inquire with Staff: Ask your floor managers, receptionists, or technicians if they remember assisting someone matching the review's context or detailed praise.
  • Look for Context Clues: Does the review mention a specific product, a unique service nuance, or a staff member's name? If a review mentions "Dr. Sneha explained my root canal wonderfully," you instantly know it's legitimate even if the booking was under a different name.

Most of the time, a little investigative work reveals the connection. However, if the review is completely generic—such as a blank 5-star rating or a vague "Good service"—and you still cannot trace it, you need a different strategy.

Step 2: Respond Professionally Without Exposing Yourself

If you genuinely cannot find the customer, your public response on Google needs to balance gratitude with subtle verification. Never write, "We don't have you in our records, who are you?" Instead, keep your tone warm, welcoming, and open-ended.

A great template to use:

"Namaste! Thank you so much for the wonderful rating. We always strive to give our best at our Bangalore center, but we want to make sure we look up your file correctly. Could you drop us a quick WhatsApp message at our business number so we can properly thank you and ensure your next visit comes with a special loyalty perk?" — Standard Best-Practice Response for Unverified Reviews

This approach does two things. First, it invites the genuine customer to step forward and connect with you directly. Second, if it is a spam account or a bot, it gracefully redirects the conversation off the public forum while signaling to prospective customers that you actively care about client satisfaction.

Handling Negative Reviews from Unknown Entities

While an unverified 5-star review is a pleasant mystery, an unverified 1-star or 2-star review from a ghost customer is an emergency. Competitors, disgruntled ex-employees, or internet trolls occasionally target local businesses with baseless negative ratings.

If you cannot find the customer in your records and they have left a damaging negative review, your action plan must be swift and methodical:

  1. Respond Calmly: Write a polite public reply stating: "We take our service quality very seriously at our Delhi outlet, but we cannot locate any record of your visit under this name. Please WhatsApp us your invoice details so we can investigate immediately."
  2. Flag for Removal: Go to your Google Business Profile dashboard and flag the review for violating Google’s guidelines regarding fake engagement or conflict of interest.
  3. Gather Evidence: If you suspect a malicious competitor, document the lack of transaction records and use Google's support channels to appeal the review effectively.

Final Thoughts

Finding an unknown name in your review notification tray doesn't have to cause anxiety. By checking your secondary ledgers, responding with tactful curiosity, and upgrading your feedback collection to verified channels, you protect your hard-earned local reputation.
