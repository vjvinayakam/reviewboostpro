What Should I Reply to a Customer Who Posts False Information About My Business?

RB
ReviewBoostPRO Editorial Team
• August 14, 2026 • 7 min read

Picture this: It is a busy Saturday evening at your restaurant in Mumbai, or perhaps you are running a bustling dental clinic in Bangalore. You open your Google Business Profile to check how things are looking, and your heart drops. A 1-star review is sitting right at the top. The user claims something completely untrue—perhaps alleging they found a foreign object in their food, or that your clinic overcharged them by ₹5,000 for a routine consultation.

Panic sets in. Your immediate instinct might be to fire back in anger, call them a liar, or threaten legal action. Stop right there. How you reply to a false review on Google is not just a message to the disgruntled user; it is a public performance watched by hundreds of prospective local customers.

In the Indian local business ecosystem, consumers are smart. They know that trolls and malicious competitors exist. However, they judge your business entirely by how professionally you handle adversity. Let's break down the exact playbook for identifying, addressing, and neutralizing false information on your Google listing.

Step 1: Pause, Breathe, and Verify Your Records

Before typing a single word of your reply, take a deep breath. Check your internal systems immediately. Look up your appointment books, billing software, CCTV footage, or customer logs for the date and time mentioned in the review.

Often, you will find one of two things:

  • A case of mistaken identity: The reviewer might have visited a similar shop down the street in Delhi or Pune and accidentally left the review on your profile.
  • A malicious competitor or bot: There is no record of this person ever stepping foot in your establishment or calling your clinic.
⚠️ The Golden Rule of Public Replies Never accuse the reviewer of lying directly in public, even if you know they are completely fabricating the story. Words like "You are lying" or "This never happened" can make your business look defensive and aggressive to third-party readers.

Step 2: Write a Fact-Based, Professional Response

Your public response has one primary objective: to show future customers that your business is mature, organized, and deeply committed to genuine customer satisfaction. Even if you know the review is fake, your tone must remain calm and objective.

Here is a proven template you can adapt for your Indian business:

"Hello [Name], we take matters of service quality and billing transparency extremely seriously at our clinic/restaurant. However, after thoroughly checking our patient/customer logs, bills, and appointment records for the past month, we cannot find any record of a transaction or visit under your name. We want to investigate this immediately to ensure no lapse occurred. Could you please reach out directly to our manager at [Phone Number] or [Email] with your receipt details so we can resolve this right away?" — Standard Professional Response Template

Why does this work so effectively? Because it achieves three things simultaneously:

  • It politely signals to anyone reading the review that this person's account cannot be verified in your system.
  • It demonstrates your willingness to solve genuine problems proactively.
  • It provides zero fuel for the reviewer to start an aggressive online argument with you.

Step 3: Flag the Review for Removal with Google

While managing the public optics is crucial, you do not have to live with defamatory or completely fake reviews forever. Google has clear content policies regarding harassment, fake engagement, and spam.

Once you have replied publicly, navigate to your Google Business Profile dashboard:

  • Locate the specific review in question.
  • Click on the three vertical dots next to the review.
  • Select "Report review" or "Flag as inappropriate".
  • Choose the correct violation category (e.g., Conflict of Interest, Spam, or Misrepresentation).

Keep in mind that Google's automated moderation can take anywhere from a few days to a couple of weeks. If the review clearly violates guidelines—such as using abusive language or being posted by a known competitor—Google will often remove it.

If you have 10 total reviews and one is a malicious 1-star fake review, your rating drops to a painful 4.0. But if you have 150 reviews, that same fake review is rendered virtually invisible.

Step 5: When to Consider Legal Action

Ninety-nine percent of fake reviews can be handled through polite replies, Google flagging, and review dilution. However, in rare instances—such as targeted defamation campaigns by former employees or rival business owners causing massive financial damage—you may need to consult a legal professional.

Indian IT laws and civil defamation provisions permit businesses to send formal legal notices for malicious online defamation designed to harm commercial standing. Ensure you preserve screenshots, IP logs, and any WhatsApp chats leading up to the incident before speaking with an advocate.

Summary Checklist for Handling False Reviews

When false information rears its head on your Google listing, follow this step-by-step checklist:

  • Do not panic or rage-reply: Anger online always harms the business owner, never the troll.
  • Verify internally: Check whether it's a mix-up or a malicious fabrication.
  • Post a calm, fact-based reply: State that records show no matching customer transaction and invite them to call privately.
  • Flag to Google: Submit a formal report for policy violation.
  • Drown it out with volume: Use tools like ReviewBoostPRO to systematically gather authentic WhatsApp reviews from your real daily customers.

By keeping your cool and maintaining a steady stream of glowing reviews from genuine patrons, false information loses its power over your local reputation entirely.
