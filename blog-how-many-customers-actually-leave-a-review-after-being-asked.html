How Many Customers Actually Leave a Review After Being Asked?

RB
ReviewBoostPRO Team
• August 2026 • 7 min read

Every local business owner in India—from bustling dental clinics in Mumbai to trendy salons in Bangalore, and busy auto repair shops in Pune—knows the undeniable power of Google reviews. When a prospective customer searches for "best dermatologist near me" or "reliable mechanic in Chennai," they don't look at billboards. They look at star ratings and recent reviews.

Recognizing this, you probably tell your front desk staff to remind every single customer, patient, or client: "Sir/Ma'am, please leave us a 5-star review on Google!"

Or perhaps you’ve tried printing QR codes on table tents, billing counters, or visiting cards. You hand over the bill, point to the glossy cardboard sign, and hope for the best.

So, let’s talk about the hard truth. Out of every 100 customers you ask face-to-face or via traditional methods, how many actually pull out their phones and write that review?

The Disappointing Reality of Manual Review Asks

If you rely purely on verbal requests at the checkout counter, your actual conversion rate typically hovers around a depressing 2% to 5%.

Think about the customer journey. Rajesh finishes his haircut at a salon in Bandra, Mumbai. The stylist smiles and says, "Please drop a review!" Rajesh nods, genuinely meaning to do it. But the moment he steps out onto the noisy street, answers a work call on WhatsApp, and hails an auto-rickshaw, your request vanishes from his mind forever.

Even if you hand over a printed QR code flyer with the bill at a busy family restaurant in Hyderabad, friction kills conversion. Scanning a QR code requires opening a camera app, waiting for a browser to redirect, logging into a Google account (which often prompts a forgotten password reset), and finally typing out feedback. For a busy consumer, that is simply too many steps.

The Friction Trap Every extra step between your request and the Google review page drops your completion rate by 50%. If a customer has to log into Google on their mobile browser, 95% of them will bounce immediately.

Why Email Requests Fail Miserably in India

Many western marketing playbooks suggest sending an automated email request 24 hours after a service or purchase. For Indian local businesses, this strategy yields near-zero results.

Consider the average Indian inbox. It is completely flooded with promotional alerts from Swiggy, Zomato, Flipkart, Amazon, banks, and utility bills. Your polite review request email from your local pharmacy or diagnostic center in Delhi gets buried in the "Promotions" tab or completely ignored.

Email open rates for local service businesses in India rarely cross 12%, and click-through rates to Google Maps struggle to stay above 1%. If you are spending time setting up automated email campaigns for reviews, you are shouting into an empty room.

For medical clinics, sending the message 2 hours after the consultation (when the patient has reached home and taken their prescribed medicine) yields the highest gratitude response.
