How to Respond to a Review From a Former Employee on Google

RB
ReviewBoostPRO Editorial Team
• August 2026 • 8 min read

Picture this: You run a busy multi-specialty dental clinic in Andheri, Mumbai, or a popular family restaurant in Indiranagar, Bangalore. You check your Google Business Profile dashboard over your morning filter coffee, expecting to see glowing feedback from patients or diners. Instead, you spot a stinging 1-star rating. You open it up, and your heart sinks. It is not from an unhappy customer. It is from a receptionist, chef, or technician who left your employment last month under less-than-friendly circumstances.

For Indian business owners, receiving an internal grievance aired publicly on Google Maps can feel deeply personal and infuriating. After all, your Google Business Profile is meant for customers, patients, and clients—not disgruntled ex-staff members airing internal office politics or wage disputes.

How you handle this delicate situation can make the difference between protecting your local reputation and triggering a public online PR disaster. In this comprehensive guide, we will break down exactly how to navigate reviews from former employees, what you can legally or practically do, and how to draft a response that reassures future customers.

Why Former Employees Leave Google Reviews

When an employee departs—whether they resigned voluntarily or were let go due to performance issues or policy violations—emotions run high. In India’s competitive local business sectors like hospitality, healthcare, and retail, staff transitions can sometimes turn messy. Common catalysts for a rogue ex-employee review include:

  • Unresolved Full & Final (F&F) Settlement disputes: Disagreements over notice period deductions, pending salary payouts, or incentive calculations.
  • Disciplinary action or termination: An employee who was fired for attendance issues or unprofessional behavior retaliates online to save face.
  • Workplace disagreements: Clashes with management, shift timing grievances, or denied leave requests.

Regardless of the motivation, you must remember one cardinal rule of online reputation management: Your response is not meant for the ex-employee. It is meant for prospective customers and patients who are browsing your Google listing to decide whether they should trust your business with their time and money.

The Golden Rule Never get into a tit-for-tat argument in the review comments. Arguing about attendance records, salary structures, or internal warnings makes your business look unprofessional to prospective clients reading the thread.

Step 1: Can You Get Google to Remove It?

Before typing out a public reply, check if the review violates Google’s Content Policies. Google does not strictly prohibit former employees from leaving reviews, but their policies do ban certain types of content. You can flag and request removal if the review contains:

  • Conflicts of interest: Reviews left by current or former employees specifically designed to manipulate a competitor's rating or retaliate against an employer can sometimes be flagged under conflict of interest guidelines, though Google's automated systems rarely catch these easily.
  • Harassment or personal attacks: If the review singles out specific managers or colleagues by name with abusive language, defamatory claims, or threats.
  • Confidential information: Breaching patient medical privacy (in clinics or pharmacies) or proprietary business secrets.

Log into your Google Business Profile dashboard, locate the review, click the three dots, and select "Report review". However, keep in mind that Google's review removal process can take days or weeks, and many legitimate-looking negative reviews from ex-staff are upheld by Google because they are framed as "customer experience" or "workplace safety" feedback.

Step 2: Crafting the Perfect Professional Response

If Google does not remove the review, or if you want to address it immediately to minimize damage, you must write a calm, factual, and polite response. Your goals are simple: remain dignified, state clearly that your establishment prioritizes customer service, and take the conversation offline.

Here are templates tailored for Indian local businesses:

Template for Service Businesses (Clinics, Salons, Repair Shops) "Hello [Name], we take all feedback regarding our workplace standards and customer care very seriously. However, we have no record of you ever being a patient/customer at our clinic. Our team is dedicated to maintaining high professional standards for everyone who walks through our doors. If you have any operational concerns to discuss, please reach out directly to our management at management@[yourbusiness].in."

Notice how this template subtly signals to anyone reading the review that the reviewer was likely never a paying customer, while maintaining a courteous, non-defensive tone. Prospective customers will immediately read between the lines.

"When a disgruntled former staff member left a 1-star review claiming our salon was disorganized, I wanted to reply with our attendance register. My business coach stopped me. Using a calm, polite response that clarified we serve hundreds of happy clients monthly completely neutralized the impact." — Rajesh Sharma, Salon Owner, Pune

Step 4: Internal HR Best Practices to Prevent Future Reviews

Prevention is always better than cure. While you cannot control every individual's reaction to termination or resignation, you can minimize the chances of public backlash by tightening your internal human resources practices:

  • Clear Exit Interviews: Conduct structured exit discussions where departing staff can air grievances privately in a safe environment. When employees feel heard before they leave, they are far less likely to vent online.
  • Prompt Full & Final Settlements: In India, delayed F&F settlements are the number one trigger for financial frustration that spills over onto public review platforms. Process clearances transparently and on time.
  • Professional Offboarding: Ensure every departure—even termination—is handled with legal compliance, proper documentation, and minimal personal hostility.

Catching an ex-employee review within the first hour allows you to post a stabilizing response before prospective local clients take notice.
