What Should I Reply to a Customer Who Says My Business Was Closed?

RB
ReviewBoostPRO Editorial Team
• August 2026 • 7 min read

Imagine this scenario: It is a bustling Saturday afternoon in South Mumbai. Your clinic, salon, or retail pharmacy is fully staffed, lights are blazing, and customers are walking in and out. Later that evening, you open your Google Business Profile dashboard and see a stinging 1-star review: "Travelled all the way from Andheri during their listed hours, but the shutter was down. Complete waste of time and petrol!"

Your blood pressure spikes. You know for a fact that your business was wide open. How could this happen? More importantly, how should you respond publicly without sounding defensive, aggressive, or indifferent?

In the Indian local business ecosystem, where word-of-mouth and online ratings dictate whether a customer walks through your doors or drives past to your competitor, handling a "false closure" review incorrectly can cost you thousands of rupees in lost daily revenue. Let’s break down how to handle these frustrating situations with absolute professionalism.

1. Don't Panic and Never Attack the Customer

Your immediate instinct when accused of being closed when you were open is to fire back: "You are lying! We were open and CCTV footage proves it!" Do not do this. Future customers reading that review won't see your righteous indignation; they will only see a rude business owner.

Instead, take a deep breath. Customers rarely leave false closure reviews out of pure malice. Most of the time, genuine confusion has occurred:

  • They may have visited during a temporary lunch break shuttering (common in tier-2 and tier-3 cities across Pune, Jaipur, and Ahmedabad).
  • They might have entered the wrong lane or visited a similarly named establishment in a crowded commercial complex.
  • They could have visited on a national holiday or regional festival day (like Ganeshotsav in Maharashtra or Pongal in Tamil Nadu) when your operating hours were modified.
The Golden Rule Never call a customer a liar in a public review response. Treat their confusion as a genuine customer service touchpoint that prospective clients are silently judging.

2. Crafting the Professional Response: Templates That Work

Your public reply serves two masters: the unhappy individual who left the review, and the hundreds of prospective patrons in Delhi, Bangalore, or Hyderabad who will read your response over the next six months. Here are three effective templates based on the specific situation:

Scenario A: Sincere and apologetic (Best for clearing up minor confusion)
"Hello Rajesh, we sincerely apologize for your frustrating experience. We are definitely operational during our regular hours (9 AM to 8 PM), so we are deeply concerned to hear that you found our doors closed. We would love to make it up to you. Please drop us a direct message or call our manager directly at [Phone Number] so we can offer you a complimentary consultation/service on your next visit."

Scenario B: Fact-checking politely (Best if a holiday or lunch hour caused the mishap)
"Dear Priya, thank you for bringing this to our attention. Our standard operating hours are Monday through Saturday, 10 AM to 7 PM. However, if you visited during our brief staff lunch break or on account of the local holiday, we deeply regret the missed connection. We welcome you to visit us again soon—mention this note at the counter for a special discount on your bill."

3. Fix the Root Cause on Your Google Business Profile

Once you have replied professionally, look inward. Why did the customer think you were closed? In many cases, the fault lies in digital miscommunication rather than physical reality.

Check your Google Business Profile right now. Are your special hours updated for upcoming Indian festivals like Diwali, Eid, or Independence Day? Did you accidentally mark a Sunday as closed when you actually operate half-days? Keeping your digital storefront accurate prevents these misunderstandings before they turn into negative ratings.

"Before we started using automated tools to keep our customers happy and engaged, a single bad review about a supposed closure would ruin our whole week. Responding calmly and collecting positive reviews instantly drowned out those rare hiccups." — Vikram Malhotra, Dental Clinic Owner, New Delhi

4. Drown Out Single Bad Reviews with Volume

Even if you handle a false closure review with the grace of a seasoned diplomat, a 1-star rating can drag down your overall score if you only have a dozen total reviews. The ultimate antidote to occasional unfair reviews is an overwhelming wave of positive feedback from your genuinely happy daily customers.

This is where streamlined operations make all the difference. When you automate your feedback collection right after a successful checkout or consultation, you build a massive buffer of 5-star ratings. 5. When and How to Flag Reviews to Google

Sometimes, competitors or spam accounts leave malicious reviews claiming your business was closed permanently or temporarily just to harm your local SEO rankings. If a review violates Google's content policies (for instance, if it's clearly spam or left by someone who has never stepped into your establishment in Chennai or Kolkata), do not hesitate to flag it.

Go to your Google Business Profile dashboard, locate the review, click the three vertical dots, and select "Report review". While Google's automated moderation can take time, clear-cut violations are frequently removed. However, always post a professional public reply first—never leave a negative review unanswered while waiting for Google to review your removal request.

Wrapping Up

Dealing with a customer who claims your business was closed is an inevitable rite of passage for local business owners. By staying calm, replying with empathy and clarity, auditing your online operating hours, and continuously flooding your profile with genuine customer love, you turn a potential PR disaster into proof of your exceptional customer service.
