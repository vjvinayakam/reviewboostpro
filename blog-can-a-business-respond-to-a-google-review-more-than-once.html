Can a Business Respond to a Google Review More Than Once?

RB
Rahul Verma
• August 14, 2026 • 7 min read

Managing a local business profile on Google in bustling Indian markets like Mumbai, Delhi, Bangalore, or Pune is no small feat. Every single day, local entrepreneurs juggle customer service, inventory, staff management, and online reputation. Among these tasks, handling Google Business Profile reviews has become one of the most critical drivers of local footfall and trust.

Picture this common scenario: A customer leaves a detailed 1-star review complaining about a long waiting time at your diagnostic clinic in Chennai or a delayed food delivery from your restaurant in Hyderabad. In a rush, your manager replies defensively or too hastily. Ten minutes later, you realize the tone was completely wrong, or perhaps the customer later visited your establishment, apologized, and resolved the issue. Naturally, you panic and ask yourself: Can I reply to this review again? Or can I edit what I already wrote?

Let's dive deep into how Google handles business responses, what you are technically allowed to do, and the best strategies to fix an inadequate or outdated review reply.

The Short Answer: No Multiple Replies, But Yes to Editing

Google’s platform design is structured to keep conversations clean and manageable. Because of this, a business cannot post multiple, separate response comments under a single customer review. You only get one official response block per review.

However, you are not trapped by your first draft. Google gives business owners the absolute freedom to edit your existing response as many times as you want. Whenever you update your reply, Google seamlessly replaces the old text with your new one. Customers and prospective buyers browsing your profile will only ever see your most recent, updated version.

Pro Tip for Local Businesses Even though you can edit your replies endlessly, avoid changing a review response drastically without reason. Frequent, erratic changes can sometimes look confusing if regular customers are following an ongoing service resolution.

How to Edit Your Existing Google Review Response

If you need to update a reply because new information has surfaced, or because you want to soften a hasty response, the process is straightforward:

  • Log into your Google Business Profile dashboard through your computer or use the Google Maps mobile application on your smartphone.
  • Navigate to the Reviews section to see all customer feedback.
  • Locate the specific review where you previously posted a response.
  • Click or tap on the three vertical dots next to your existing published response.
  • Select Edit, type in your refined, polished message, and hit save. Your updated response will go live almost instantly.

When Should You Update Your Review Response?

Knowing *when* to edit your response is just as important as knowing *how*. Here are three distinct scenarios where updating your reply is a smart business move:

1. The Issue Has Been Resolved Offline: If a customer complained about a billing error at your pharmacy or a faulty repair at your auto workshop, and you successfully solved the issue in person or over a phone call, update your response. Politely mention that the matter has been resolved and invite them back.

2. The Customer Edits Their Original Review: Often, once you address a customer's grievance, they will graciously update their 2-star review to a 5-star rating. When this happens, your response should immediately reflect your gratitude for their updated feedback.

3. You Regret a Hasty, Emotional Reply: Business owners are human. If an unfair or abusive review provoked an emotional response that doesn't align with your brand standards, edit it immediately. Replace it with a calm, professional, and courteous statement.

Best Practices for Writing Professional Google Review Responses

Whether you are writing your initial response or editing an old one, keep these golden rules in mind:

  • Personalize the greeting: Use the customer’s name rather than a generic "Dear Customer."
  • Keep it concise: Avoid writing long essays. Get straight to the point and show appreciation or offer a solution.
  • Take offline issues offline: For complex complaints, provide a direct phone number or email address so the customer can talk to a manager privately.
  • Maintain a polite tone: Always remain calm and courteous, keeping in mind that hundreds of future customers are reading your reply.
