Should You Send Google Review Requests by SMS or WhatsApp?

RB
ReviewBoostPRO Editorial Team
• August 2026 • 7 min read

If you run a local business in India—whether it is a busy dental clinic in Mumbai, a popular family restaurant in Bangalore, a premium salon in Delhi, or an auto repair workshop in Pune—you already know how vital Google reviews are. When a prospective customer searches for your services on their smartphone, your star rating and review count can make or break their decision to walk through your doors or book your services.

The real question for business owners today isn't whether they should ask for reviews, but how they should ask. For over a decade, traditional SMS was the default channel for automated customer follow-ups and review link distribution. However, in modern India, the communication landscape has shifted dramatically. Nearly every smartphone user relies on WhatsApp for daily personal and professional interactions.

So, which channel actually gets your local business more 5-star Google ratings: SMS or WhatsApp? Let's dive deep into the data, consumer behavior, and operational realities across Indian cities to find out.

The Current State of SMS Marketing in India

Traditional SMS has been the workhorse of transactional and promotional alerts. When a patient checks out of a clinic or a customer completes a payment at a pharmacy, an SMS gateway fires off a digital receipt alongside a short link.

However, SMS faces massive headwinds in the Indian market today:

  • Inbox Clutter and Spam Filters: The average smartphone user in Delhi or Hyderabad receives dozens of promotional text messages daily from banks, e-commerce giants, and telemarketers. Most of these folders are flooded, leading users to ignore their SMS inbox entirely.
  • Strict DND (Do Not Disturb) Regulations: TRAI guidelines and telecom operators have clamped down heavily on commercial messaging. Legitimate businesses often struggle with message delivery failures, sender ID blocks, and complex template approvals.
  • Zero Interactivity: SMS is a one-way street. If a customer has a query or encounters an issue with their review link, replying to a shortcode or standard text number is cumbersome and often goes unanswered.
The SMS Reality Check Open rates for standard promotional and transactional SMS hover around 15% to 20% in urban India, and click-through rates for review links rarely cross 3% to 4%. For busy local businesses spending ₹2,000 to ₹5,000 monthly on SMS packs, the ROI is rapidly declining.

Why WhatsApp Has Become India's Preferred Business Channel

WhatsApp is not just an app in India; it is the operating system of social and commercial life. From ordering groceries on quick-commerce apps to booking cab rides and consulting doctors, Indian consumers expect businesses to meet them where they already spend hours every day.

When you evaluate WhatsApp for gathering Google reviews, the advantages are stark:

    Unmatched Open Rates: WhatsApp messages consistently achieve open rates exceeding 95%, with the vast majority being read within minutes of delivery. When a customer walks out of your salon in Chennai or picks up their prescription at a pharmacy in Kolkata, a WhatsApp notification immediately grabs their attention.

    Personal and Conversational Tone: Unlike a cold, automated SMS, a WhatsApp message feels warm, human, and approachable. It allows businesses to use rich formatting, emojis, and direct call-to-action buttons that make leaving a review effortless.

    Frictionless Tap-to-Review Experience: WhatsApp natively supports hyperlinks and interactive buttons. A customer simply taps the message, is instantly redirected to your Google Business Profile review dialog, and can submit their star rating in under ten seconds.

Head-to-Head Comparison: SMS vs. WhatsApp for Review Requests

To make an informed decision for your establishment, let's look at how both channels stack up across key performance indicators:

  • Delivery Reliability: SMS suffers from carrier filtering and DND blocks. WhatsApp business APIs ensure high deliverability rates when templates are pre-approved.
  • Engagement Speed: While SMS might sit unread for hours or days, WhatsApp notifications trigger immediate visual and auditory alerts on lock screens.
  • Conversion Rate: Local businesses utilizing ReviewBoostPRO often see review generation conversion rates jump from 4% on SMS to over 35% when switching to automated WhatsApp review flows.
  • Cost-Efficiency: While WhatsApp conversational API messages carry a fractional per-message cost, the significantly higher volume of earned reviews translates to a much lower cost per acquired 5-star rating.
Pro Tip for Local Business Owners Timing is everything. Send your review request via WhatsApp within 30 to 60 minutes of the customer completing their transaction or visit, while the positive experience is still fresh in their mind.

Addressing Potential Concerns: Is WhatsApp Compliant?

Many business owners wonder about WhatsApp spam policies. It is important to note that sending unverified bulk spam through personal WhatsApp numbers can lead to account bans. However, using official WhatsApp Business API platforms ensures 100% compliance.

When you collect customer phone numbers at your billing counter or appointment desk with explicit consent, sending transactional follow-up messages requesting feedback fully adheres to Meta's guidelines. Furthermore, automated tools help manage opt-outs seamlessly, protecting your brand reputation.

The Verdict: Which One Should You Choose?

While SMS still holds a secondary role for urgent transactional OTPs and banking alerts, it is no longer an effective primary channel for marketing engagement or review generation in India.

If your goal is to rank higher in Google Maps local pack results, outshine your local competitors in Mumbai, Delhi, Bangalore, or Jaipur, and turn casual walk-ins into loyal brand advocates, WhatsApp is the undisputed winner. Its unmatched open rates, superior user experience, and conversational familiarity make it the ultimate engine for modern reputation management.
