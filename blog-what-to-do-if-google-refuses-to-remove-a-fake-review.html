What to Do If Google Refuses to Remove a Fake Review

RB
ReviewBoostPRO Editorial Team
• August 14, 2026 • 7 min read

Picture this: You arrive at your dental clinic in Bandra, Mumbai, or your popular family restaurant in Indiranagar, Bangalore, ready to tackle the day. You open your Google Business Profile, and your heart drops. A fresh 1-star review from an absolute stranger claims your service was atrocious, your staff was rude, or worse—that they fell ill after visiting your establishment.

You check your appointment registers, CCTV footage, and billing software. There is zero record of this person. Naturally, you flag the review as fraudulent through the Google Business Profile Help community or the management dashboard, expecting Google’s automated system or human moderators to see reason and take it down.

A few days later, the notification arrives: "We've reviewed your request, and the review does not violate our policies."

Panic sets in. For Indian local business owners who rely heavily on local search visibility, a stubborn 1-star rating can instantly wipe out hard-earned trust and cost tens of thousands of rupees in lost monthly revenue. If Google has rejected your initial appeal, it is easy to feel entirely helpless. However, a rejected removal request is not the end of the road. Here is your definitive, step-by-step playbook on what to do next.

1. Understand Why Google Rejected Your Appeal

Google’s automated moderation tools and support agents handle millions of review flags daily. Their threshold for "clear policy violation" is notoriously strict. Google assumes that consumers tell the truth unless a review explicitly violates specific guidelines—such as containing hate speech, blatant spam patterns, personal phone numbers, or conflicts of interest.

If a fake reviewer leaves a vague 1-star rating without offensive language, Google’s system reads it as a legitimate customer grievance. Knowing this helps you shift your strategy from begging Google to delete it, to neutralizing its impact and building a stronger secondary case.

Critical Mistake to Avoid Do not reply to the fake review in anger, threaten legal action, or use insulting language. Future genuine customers will read your response. If you sound aggressive, potential clients will side with the reviewer, regardless of whether the review is fake or not.

2. Craft a Smart, Public-Facing Response

Even if Google refuses to delete the review, that comment section is your stage. You are not writing your response for the fake reviewer—you are writing it for the prospective customers in Chennai, Delhi, or Pune who will read it next week.

Your goal is to politely expose the fact that the review is likely fraudulent while maintaining absolute professionalism. Use a template similar to this:

  • Acknowledge and Verify: "Hello [Name], we take all feedback seriously, but after checking our records across our Mumbai clinic databases, we cannot find any patient or customer matching your name or visit details."
  • Invite Direct Contact: "We pride ourselves on our transparency and service quality. Please reach out to our manager directly at [Your Business Phone/Email] so we can investigate this immediately if there has been a genuine mix-up."

When future clients see this calm, structured reply, they will instantly recognize that the review lacks credibility.

3. Escalate Through the Google Business Profile Help Community

If the standard reporting tool fails, you have one primary official recourse left: the Google Business Profile Help Forum.

Diamond Product Experts (volunteer moderators with direct escalation paths to Google engineering) monitor this forum. To get their attention, you need to present an airtight case:

  • Create a new post in the official Google Business Profile Help Community.
  • Provide your exact Business Profile link and Case ID numbers from your previous rejected removal requests.
  • Attach clear screenshots and factual evidence demonstrating why the review violates terms (e.g., if the reviewer is a local competitor with a conflicting business, or if they have left 50 identical 1-star reviews across different cities in one day).

Keep your tone objective and professional. If a Product Expert agrees that the review violates terms, they can escalate it directly to Google internal teams for a secondary manual review.

4. Bury the Fake Review with an Influx of Real Customer Voices

Mathematically, the absolute best defense against a rogue 1-star review is volume. If you have a steady stream of glowing 5-star reviews rolling in every single week, a solitary 1-star review gets buried and diluted, losing all its psychological weight on your overall rating.

5. Document Everything for Legal or Cyber Cell Action

In extreme cases where a fake review crosses the line into defamation, cyberbullying, or malicious business sabotage by a known competitor, you can explore legal avenues.

Consult a cyber law advocate in your city to draft a formal legal notice. You can also file a complaint with the Indian Cyber Crime Coordination Centre (I4C) portal if the reviews constitute targeted online harassment. While Google itself is shielded by intermediary guidelines, identifying the individual behind the fake profile through local legal pressure often forces them to delete the review themselves out of fear of civil litigation.
