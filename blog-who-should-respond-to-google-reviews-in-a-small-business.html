Who Should Respond to Google Reviews in a Small Business?

RB
ReviewBoostPRO Team
• August 2026 • 7 min read

Imagine walking into a bustling multi-specialty clinic in Mumbai or a popular family-run restaurant in Pune. A customer walks up to the front desk, praises the food or expresses frustration about a delayed appointment, and the staff completely ignores them. Unthinkable, right? Yet, thousands of Indian local businesses commit this exact digital sin every single day on Google Maps and Google Business Profiles.

When a customer leaves a review, they are not just typing into the digital ether. They are speaking directly to your brand in front of thousands of potential local shoppers. In India's hyper-competitive metropolitan markets—from Delhi and Bangalore to Chennai and Hyderabad—proactive review management can make or break your local SEO rankings.

The real question business owners ask is: Who exactly should be responsible for replying to these reviews? Should it be the busy owner working 14 hours a day, the front-desk receptionist, a hired digital marketing intern, or a specialized automation workflow?

Let's break down how to assign this critical task effectively within your team.

1. The Founder or Business Owner: When You Must Take the Wheel

In the early stages of any Indian startup, local shop, or independent dental practice, the owner wears every hat. From managing inventory to handling vendor payments in ₹, you do it all. Should you handle Google reviews?

In the absolute beginning, yes, the owner must personally respond to reviews. Doing so sets the definitive brand voice. It shows deep personal commitment to customer satisfaction. When a customer in Bangalore leaves a 5-star review for your boutique clothing store and the founder replies with a warm, customized note, it creates an immediate emotional bond.

However, as your business scales, keeping review management solely on the founder's plate becomes a severe bottleneck. When you are rushing between client meetings or managing daily operations, reviews slip through the cracks. Weeks pass by without a single response, signaling to Google and prospective customers that your listing is abandoned.

The Danger of Founder Bottleneck When the business owner tries to do everything, Google reviews become an afterthought. Unanswered reviews accumulate, angry customers feel ignored, and your local search rankings stall out.

2. Frontline Staff and Receptionists: The Ideal Frontline Responders

Who interacts with your customers in real life? Your front-desk executive at a diagnostic center in Hyderabad, your salon manager in Chennai, or your floor supervisor at an auto repair shop in Delhi.

These team members know the customers best. They remember if Mrs. Sharma brought her car in for brake pads last Tuesday or if Mr. Verma had a root canal. Empowering your frontline staff to manage routine positive reviews builds accountability and reinforces customer-centric behavior.

However, delegation requires strict guardrails and templates:

  • Positive Reviews: Front desk or managers can reply using warm, professional templates customized with the customer's name.
  • Neutral or Negative Reviews: Frontline staff should never reply emotionally to criticism. A defensive or sarcastic reply from an overzealous employee can go viral and devastate your reputation overnight. Negative reviews must always be escalated to senior management or the owner.

3. Dedicated Marketing Managers or External Agencies

Many growing retail chains and multi-location businesses hire digital agencies or employ an in-house marketing coordinator. While they are great at running Instagram ads or optimizing your website SEO, are they the right fit for Google reviews?

Marketing agencies can draft polished, SEO-optimized responses and maintain a consistent schedule. The pitfall? They often lack operational context. If a customer complains that the AC wasn't working at your restaurant in Bandra, an external agency might reply with a generic corporate canned response like: "We regret the inconvenience. Please mail support@brand.com."

That kind of robotic response infuriates local Indian consumers who want a human, empathetic acknowledgment and a swift resolution.

4. Streamlining the Process: The Hybrid Model + Automation

The most successful businesses in India today do not rely on just one person. They use a hybrid accountability model powered by smart tools.

  • Quick Action Templates: Standardized, approved reply templates allow managers to push quick thank-you notes in seconds, while critical alerts ensure negative feedback hits the owner's phone immediately for rapid offline resolution.

Best Practices for Assigning Review Roles in Your Business

To establish a bulletproof review response protocol in your local business, follow these four foundational rules:

Define Ownership Clearly: Never leave review management as "everyone's job." When everyone is responsible, no one does it. Assign specific shifts or specific managers to check and clear the review queue daily.

Set a Response SLA: Aim to respond to every single Google review within 24 hours. Google's algorithm rewards active listing engagement, and prospective customers notice how attentive you are.

Train Your Team on Tone: Ensure your staff understands that every reply represents the owner's reputation. Keep tones polite, localized, and grateful.

Leverage Modern Tools: Stop wasting hours logging into desktop browsers. Empower your team with ReviewBoostPRO to manage review generation, sentiment tracking, and response workflows seamlessly from your mobile phone.

By clarifying who responds, setting clear boundaries on negative feedback, and equipping your team with streamlined mobile tools, your Google Business Profile will transform from a passive directory listing into your most powerful local customer acquisition engine.
