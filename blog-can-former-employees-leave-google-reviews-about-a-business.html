Can Former Employees Leave Google Reviews About a Business?

RB
ReviewBoostPRO Team
• August 2026 • 7 min read

Picture this scenario: You run a bustling multi-specialty dental clinic in Bandra, Mumbai, or a popular upscale salon chain in Bangalore's Indiranagar. Business is steady, and you rely heavily on your Google Business Profile to attract new local foot traffic. One morning, you open your Google dashboard to check your ratings and spot a stinging 1-star review.

When you click on the reviewer's profile, your heart sinks. It is written by a disgruntled receptionist or a technician you terminated last week due to attendance issues. Immediately, a wave of panic and frustration sets in. Is this allowed? Can former employees legally and legitimately trash your business reputation on Google? Can they write reviews about a place they used to work for?

This is one of the most common and stressful dilemmas faced by Indian business owners across sectors like healthcare, hospitality, automotive repair, and retail. Let us break down what Google’s official guidelines say, how Indian laws intersect with online reputation, and what actionable steps you can take to protect your local business.

What Does Google's Official Policy Say About Employee Reviews?

To understand whether former employees can review your business, we must look directly at Google’s Restricted Content policies for Google Maps and Google Business Profile. Google has strict rules regarding conflicts of interest. According to Google:

  • Conflict of Interest: Maps content should reflect unbiased information. Owners, managers, and current employees are explicitly prohibited from reviewing their own workplaces or competing businesses to artificially inflate or deflate ratings.
  • Representing the Customer Experience: Google reviews are fundamentally designed to help prospective consumers understand the authentic customer or patient experience. An internal employment dispute is not a consumer transaction.
  • Bias and Retaliation: Google’s automated moderation systems and human review teams penalize content posted out of spite, retaliation, or personal vendettas following termination or resignation.

Therefore, current employees cannot leave genuine customer reviews for their own establishment. But what about former employees? The line gets slightly blurred here, but Google's underlying philosophy remains clear: reviews must be based on a genuine first-hand customer or visitor experience, not an internal HR grievance.

The Google Policy Reality Check While a former employee who also happened to buy a product or use a service at your clinic or restaurant as a paying customer technically has a right to review that specific transaction, using a review platform to air internal workplace complaints, wage disputes, or management clashes is a direct violation of Google's prohibited content guidelines regarding harassment and conflict of interest.

Why Do Ex-Employees Target Google Business Profiles?

Local businesses in fast-growing metropolitan hubs like Delhi, Chennai, Pune, and Hyderabad operate in highly competitive markets. A drop of even 0.2 stars on Google can significantly impact local search rankings and consumer trust. Disgruntled former staff members know this vulnerability all too well.

Common motivations behind former employee review bombing include:

  • Retaliation over Termination: Firing an underperforming staff member or denying an unreasonable salary hike often triggers emotional outbursts.
  • Unpaid Dues or FNF Disputes: Disagreements over Full and Final (FnF) settlements or delayed commissions sometimes push frustrated workers to attack public assets.
  • Malicious Sabotage: In worst-case scenarios, competing local businesses or unscrupulous agencies might orchestrate fake negative reviews using dummy accounts posing as ex-workers.

When negative reviews stem from personal malice rather than actual service failures, it damages your brand equity and costs you real customers who might have spent ₹500 or ₹50,000 at your establishment.

How to Identify and Prove an Employee Review

Before you take any action, you need to verify whether the reviewer is indeed a former staff member. Look for these telltale signs in the review text and profile:

  • Inside Terminology: Mentions of internal management names, back-office policies, shift timings, or locker room conditions that normal customers would never observe.
  • Focus on Employment: Phrases like "Worst management I've ever worked under," "They don't clear salaries on time," or "Toxic work culture."
  • Anonymous or Ghost Profiles: Profiles with no prior review history, default avatars, or generic names designed to conceal identity.

If the review explicitly discusses working conditions rather than the quality of your services or products, you have strong grounds to challenge it.

Step-by-Step Guide to Removing a Former Employee's Review

If an ex-employee has left a biased or defamatory review, do not panic and do not reply with an angry, emotional rant in the public comment section. Follow this structured protocol instead:

1. Flag the Review Through Google Support

Log into your Google Business Profile dashboard, locate the review, click the three dots menu next to it, and select "Report review". Choose the appropriate violation category, such as "Conflict of Interest" or "Spam/Harassment." Google usually processes these requests within 3 to 7 business days.

2. Submit a Support Ticket via the Management Tool

If the automated flagging does not work, visit the Google Business Profile Help Community or the Review Management Tool. Provide clear context explaining that the reviewer is a terminated employee violating conflict-of-interest policies.

3. Respond Professionally for Future Customers

While you wait for Google to remove the review, post a calm, professional response. Your goal is not to argue with the ex-employee, but to reassure prospective customers reading the thread.

Example Professional Response Template "Hello [Name], we pride ourselves on maintaining the highest standards of service and professionalism for all our clients and team members. We have no record of you ever visiting our establishment as a customer. If you have any operational feedback, our management team is always reachable directly at management@[yourbusiness].in."

This approach signals to anyone reading the review that the poster is likely an internal actor rather than a dissatisfied customer.

Legal Options for Defamation in India

In extreme cases where a former employee posts defamatory, malicious lies that cause severe financial loss to your business, Indian law provides protections under both civil and criminal frameworks.

Under Section 356 of the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita (BNS)—which replaced Section 499 of the Indian Penal Code (IPC)—criminal defamation applies to anyone who publishes false statements intended to harm the reputation of a person or business. Furthermore, under the Information Technology (IT) Act, posting malicious digital content can attract legal scrutiny.

However, pursuing formal legal action through courts can be time-consuming and expensive. For 95% of businesses, combining Google's removal process with an aggressive strategy to collect genuine customer reviews is the fastest and most cost-effective solution.

Best Practices to Prevent Employee Review Issues

Prevention is always better than cure. You can minimize the risk of disgruntled staff venting online by implementing sound internal practices:

  • Conduct Smooth Exit Interviews: Always hold a constructive exit interview during resignations or terminations to address grievances privately before they spill onto public forums.
  • Maintain Clear HR Policies: Ensure clear documentation regarding performance warnings, disciplinary actions, and final settlements.
  • Keep Communication Open: Foster a workplace culture where staff members feel heard, reducing the emotional impulse to seek public revenge.
