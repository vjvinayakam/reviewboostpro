How to Deal With Multiple Reviews From the Same Customer

RB
ReviewBoostPRO Editorial
• August 2026 • 7 min read

Picture this: You run a busy dental clinic in Bandra, Mumbai or a popular multi-cuisine restaurant in Koramangala, Bangalore. You open your Google Business Profile dashboard, eager to check your local reputation. You spot a glowing 5-star rating from a regular client, Rajesh Sharma. But wait—scroll down a bit further, and there is another 5-star review from Rajesh Sharma posted three days later, and a third one from what appears to be his secondary Google account.

At first glance, extra positive reviews might sound like a wonderful problem to have. More stars mean higher local visibility on Google Maps, right? Not necessarily. Google’s algorithms value authenticity, relevance, and natural patterns. When multiple reviews flood in from the exact same individual or device signature, it can trigger spam filters, confuse potential customers browsing your profile, or violate Google's strict content guidelines.

Whether it is an enthusiastic regular trying to help your business grow by spamming nice notes, a disgruntled customer leaving serial 1-star rants across multiple profile entries, or simple technical glitches caused by slow mobile networks in tier-2 Indian cities, duplicate reviews need a structured approach. Let's explore why this happens and how Indian business owners can clean up their profiles without burning bridges with genuine patrons.

Why Do Customers Leave Multiple Google Reviews?

Before jumping into damage control, it helps to understand consumer psychology. In most cases, malicious intent is far down the list. Here is why multiple reviews from the same person typically show up on Indian business listings:

  • Overzealous Regulars: Loyal customers who genuinely love your service—such as a regular patient at a Hyderabad dental clinic or a loyal member at a Pune fitness studio—might click your review link every single time they visit, thinking they are racking up loyalty points or simply showing ongoing appreciation.
  • App Glitches and Network Drops: India's mobile internet connectivity can occasionally be unpredictable. A customer might tap "Post," experience a network timeout, assume the review failed to upload, and tap submit again—resulting in identical duplicate entries appearing simultaneously.
  • Multiple Accounts: Many smartphone users in India maintain separate Google accounts for personal use, office work, or YouTube channels. A customer might accidentally log in using a different Gmail ID during a subsequent visit and leave another review without realizing they already reviewed you last month.
  • Retaliatory Spam: In unfortunate cases of a local dispute—such as an auto repair shop in Chennai disagreeing with a customer over billing spares—an angry consumer might create alternate Gmail profiles to flood your listing with negative ratings.
The Danger of Ignoring Duplicates Google's automated spam detection is powerful. If an algorithm notices a cluster of reviews originating from the same device, IP address, or individual profile associated with your business, it may flag your entire Google Business Profile for suspicious activity, suppressing your local map rankings entirely.

Step 1: Check If the Reviews Are Positive or Negative

Your action plan depends entirely on the sentiment and impact of the extra reviews. Not all duplicate reviews require an aggressive takedown request.

If a delighted customer leaves two positive reviews a few weeks apart because they visited twice and loved both experiences, the stakes are low. However, having duplicate positive text can look unnatural to eagle-eyed consumers who browse your reviews before booking an appointment or reserving a table. On the flip side, multiple negative reviews from the same frustrated individual require immediate, tactical intervention to protect your brand image.

Step 2: Reach Out Politely via WhatsApp or Phone

Since the reviewer is a real customer you likely know, the most humane and effective first step is a direct, polite conversation. In India, relationships matter deeply. A harsh legal threat or public callout can backfire terribly.

If you have their contact details from your billing software or appointment register, drop them a friendly message. For instance, if you run a pharmacy chain in Delhi or a boutique salon in Ahmedabad, you can send a quick WhatsApp note:

"Hi Rajesh ji! Thank you so much for the wonderful reviews you left for our clinic. We truly appreciate your support! We noticed that a couple of duplicate entries got posted due to a network glitch. Could we request you to keep your most detailed review and delete the extra ones? It helps Google keep our page clean. Thanks a ton!" — Standard outreach template used by Indian local merchants

Most regular customers will gladly oblige once they realize their enthusiasm accidentally cluttered your profile.

Step 3: How to Flag and Report Duplicate Reviews to Google

If the reviewer refuses to remove the duplicate, or if it is an anonymous troll spamming negative ratings from fake accounts, you must rely on Google's formal removal channels.

  • Log into your Google Business Profile dashboard or open Google Maps on your desktop.
  • Navigate to your business listing and locate the specific reviews left by the customer.
  • Click the three vertical dots (menu icon) next to the redundant or spam review.
  • Select "Report review".
  • Choose the appropriate reason, such as "Spam" or "Conflict of interest" if it violates Google guidelines.

Keep in mind that Google's review moderation queue can take anywhere from a few days to a couple of weeks. Do not spam the report button multiple times for the same review, as this can reset your request status in their moderation queue.

Step 4: Craft Professional Public Replies

While you wait for Google to review your removal request, future customers are already reading your page. Therefore, your public response strategy is critical. Your reply shouldn't sound defensive or accusatory.

If it's a positive duplicate, acknowledge their ongoing loyalty warmly: "Thanks again for your continuous patronage, Rajesh! We love having you at our restaurant." If it's a negative duplicate, maintain absolute composure: "Hello, we see multiple comments regarding your recent visit. We would love to resolve your concerns directly. Please connect with our manager at..." This shows prospective clients that you handle feedback with grace and professionalism.
