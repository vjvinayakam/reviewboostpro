What Should I Reply to a Google Review From a Former Employee?

RB
ReviewBoostPRO Editorial Team
• August 14, 2026 • 7 min read

Picture this scenario: You run a bustling dental clinic in Andheri, Mumbai, or perhaps a popular family restaurant in Indiranagar, Bangalore. You open your Google Business Profile dashboard to check your latest feedback, only to find a scathing 1-star rating. But the reviewer isn't a patient who waited too long or a diner who received cold food. It’s a disgruntled former receptionist, technician, or delivery boy who was recently let go for performance issues or quit after a workplace disagreement.

In the competitive Indian local business landscape, your online reputation is everything. Prospective customers rarely read past the first few reviews before deciding whether to book an appointment at your clinic, visit your salon, or service their car at your workshop. When an ex-employee uses Google Maps as a personal venting ground or an instrument of blackmail, it can send your local SEO rankings tumbling and scare away paying clients.

So, what should you reply? Losing your temper, launching personal attacks, or dragging internal HR disputes into public view will only backfire. In this comprehensive guide, we will break down the exact strategy Indian business owners need to handle reviews from former employees gracefully, professionally, and effectively.

Why Do Former Employees Leave Negative Google Reviews?

Before crafting your response, it helps to understand the psychology behind this specific type of false or hostile review. In most cases in India, an ex-employee leaves a negative review out of wounded pride, frustration over unpaid final settlements, or resentment after being terminated. They know that your Google rating directly impacts your revenue, making it a weapon of choice for a disgruntled individual.

Common grievances cited in these reviews include:

  • Exaggerated or completely fabricated claims about toxic workplace culture or management behavior.
  • False allegations regarding salary delays, withheld bonuses, or Provident Fund (PF) deposits.
  • Misleading statements about the quality of services or products offered by the business, claiming "they cheat customers."
  • Personal vendettas targeting the business owner or manager by name.
The Golden Rule of Public Replies Never argue, use sarcastic language, or disclose confidential employee records (such as attendance, performance metrics, or salary details) in a public Google review response. Remember: future customers are reading your reply to judge your character and professionalism, not the ex-employee's.

Step 1: Check Google's Terms of Service and Flag the Review

Before you type a single word of your reply, check if the review violates Google’s Content Policy. Google explicitly prohibits conflicts of interest and reviews intended to manipulate ratings or defame businesses maliciously. Reviews left by current or former employees often violate conflict-of-interest guidelines because they do not reflect a genuine consumer experience.

Log into your Google Business Profile dashboard, locate the review, click the three vertical dots next to it, and select "Report review." Choose the appropriate reason—such as "Conflict of Interest" or "Spam." While Google’s automated moderation can be slow, many policy-violating reviews from ex-employees get successfully removed if flagged correctly. However, never rely solely on flagging; you must post a public reply while waiting for Google's decision.

Step 2: Draft a Neutral, Fact-Based Public Response

If Google does not remove the review, your public reply serves one primary purpose: reassuring prospective customers who stumble upon it. You are not trying to win an argument with the ex-employee; you are showing future patrons that your business operates with maturity, accountability, and calm professionalism.

Your response should be polite, concise, and completely devoid of emotional outbursts. Acknowledge that you take feedback seriously, state clearly that you have no record of them as a genuine customer, and invite them to resolve the matter offline through proper channels.

Here are a few templates tailored for Indian local businesses:

Template for Service Businesses (Clinics, Salons, Repair Shops):
"Hello [Name], we take all feedback very seriously at [Business Name]. However, after checking our client database across our [City] location, we have no record of you ever visiting our establishment as a customer. If you were a former team member with a grievance regarding workplace policies, we request you to reach out directly to our HR department at [Email/Phone] so we can address your concerns privately rather than misleading our patrons."

Template for Retail & Hospitality (Restaurants, Hotels, Pharmacies):
"Dear [Name], thank you for sharing your feedback. At [Business Name], our priority is delivering exceptional experiences to every patron in [City]. We cannot locate any transaction history matching your name in our customer logs. If you have an internal administrative matter to discuss, please contact management directly at [Phone Number]."

Step 4: Know When to Consult Legal Counsel

In extreme cases, ex-employees cross the line from petty online venting into severe defamation, leaking proprietary business secrets, or posting abusive, threatening language. If the review causes substantial financial harm or damages your brand reputation in your local market, you have legal recourse under Indian law.

You can consult a cyber-law advocate to send a formal legal notice under Section 499/500 of the Indian Penal Code (IPC) for criminal defamation, or initiate civil proceedings for damages. However, litigation can be time-consuming and expensive. In 90% of cases, maintaining strict professional composure in your reply and drowning out the noise with authentic customer reviews is the fastest and most profitable strategy.

Pro-Tip for Local Business Owners Never let an ex-employee's review ruin your day or disrupt your team's morale. Treat it as a minor bump in the road, reply professionally to show future clients you are in control, and focus your energy on delivering top-notch service to your actual customers.
