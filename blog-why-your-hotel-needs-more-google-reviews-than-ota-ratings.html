Why Your Hotel Needs More Google Reviews Than OTA Ratings

RB
ReviewBoostPRO Team
· August 2026 · 8 min read

If you run a hotel in Pune, Hyderabad, or anywhere across India, you probably spend a lot of energy managing your ratings on MakeMyTrip, Goibibo, and Booking.com. And that makes sense — OTAs (Online Travel Agencies) drive a significant chunk of bookings for most properties. But here is the uncomfortable truth that most hoteliers are missing: your Google Business Profile is quietly making or breaking your reputation, and it is doing it for free, every single day, whether you are paying attention or not.

The traveller who discovers your hotel on MakeMyTrip does not stop there. They open Google, type your hotel name, and read what real guests have written. If your Google profile shows 47 reviews with a 3.9 rating while your OTA profile shows 400 reviews with a 4.3 score, which number do you think they trust more? The one that feels independent, unfiltered, and right there on Google Maps.

This article explains exactly why Google reviews deserve more of your attention than your OTA ratings — and what steps you can take starting this week.

The Search Moment That OTAs Cannot Control

Think about how most Indian travellers plan a trip today. They might start on an OTA, but the decision-making process almost always involves Google. Someone planning a family holiday in Goa or a business trip to Bangalore will search "best hotels near Whitefield" or "affordable hotels in Bandra" directly in Google Search or Google Maps. What appears at the top of those results is determined almost entirely by your Google Business Profile — and a key ranking signal is the volume and recency of your Google reviews.

OTA ratings live inside OTA platforms. They help you rank on MakeMyTrip. They do almost nothing to help you rank on Google. This is the fundamental disconnect that costs Indian hoteliers thousands of rupees in direct bookings every single month.

💡 Key Insight Google reviews directly influence your ranking in the Local Pack — those three hotel listings that appear at the very top of Google Search results with a map. More reviews, higher quality, more recent = better position. Better position = more direct bookings = zero OTA commission paid.

OTA Ratings Are Walled Gardens

Your 4.5-star rating on Booking.com is genuinely valuable. Nobody is denying that. But consider what that rating cannot do for you:

  • It does not appear when someone Googles your hotel name directly.
  • It does not influence Google Maps rankings in your city.
  • It cannot be seen by the corporate travel manager in Chennai who is booking rooms for a team offsite and searching on Google.
  • It does not help you when someone sees your hotel hoarding on the highway and looks you up immediately.
  • It is controlled by the OTA — they can change display rules, suppress reviews, or de-list you at any point.

Google reviews, on the other hand, are permanently associated with your business listing. They travel with your brand. They show up in Google Search, Google Maps, Google Assistant results, and even in the knowledge panel that appears when someone searches your exact hotel name. They are your asset, not the OTA's.

Direct Bookings vs. OTA Bookings — The Real Cost

Let us put some numbers to this. If your hotel charges ₹3,500 per night and an OTA charges 18–22% commission, you are paying ₹630–₹770 per booking to the platform. For a 30-room hotel running at 60% occupancy, that could easily mean ₹3–4 lakh in commissions every single month.

Now imagine even 15–20% of those bookings coming directly through phone calls or your website, driven by guests finding you on Google Maps. That is a meaningful saving — money that stays in your pocket, not the OTA's.

Strong Google reviews are one of the most cost-effective ways to build that direct booking channel. Guests who find you organically on Google tend to call directly, trust your own website pricing, and often become repeat customers who bypass OTAs entirely on their next visit.

⚠️ Warning for Hotel Owners If your Google Business Profile has fewer than 50 reviews, you are virtually invisible in local search compared to competitors who have 200+ reviews. In cities like Mumbai and Delhi, the bar is even higher — top-ranked hotels in competitive micro-markets often have 500–1,000+ Google reviews.

The Trust Gap Between OTA Reviews and Google Reviews

Here is something counterintuitive: travellers often trust Google reviews more than OTA reviews, even when the OTA has far more of them.

Why? Because OTA reviews are only left by people who booked through that platform. Google reviews can come from anyone — walk-in guests, event attendees, restaurant diners who visited your in-house café, corporate guests who booked directly. This diversity makes Google reviews feel more authentic and representative of the full guest experience.

Additionally, people know that businesses cannot easily game Google reviews the way they might incentivise OTA reviews through loyalty points or follow-up discount codes. The perception of independence is extremely powerful.

Why Hotels Struggle to Get Google Reviews (And How to Fix It)

Most hotels are excellent at collecting OTA reviews because OTAs send automatic post-stay emails on your behalf. The system is built in. Google reviews require you to ask guests proactively — and most hotel staff simply forget, feel awkward asking, or do not have a smooth process in place.

Here is the good news: the fix is straightforward. The secret is timing and channel. The best moment to ask for a Google review is within 2–4 hours of check-out, while the experience is fresh and the emotional warmth of a good stay is still present. And the best channel in India, by a significant margin, is WhatsApp.

Consider the numbers. Email open rates in India hover around 20–25%. WhatsApp messages are opened by more than 90% of recipients, often within minutes. When a guest receives a warm, personalised WhatsApp message from your hotel thanking them for their stay and including a direct link to leave a Google review, the conversion rate is dramatically higher than any email follow-up.

This is exactly the workflow that tools like ReviewBoostPRO are built around — helping Indian hotels send WhatsApp review requests automatically at the right moment, without your front desk staff having to remember or feel awkward about asking.

What a Strong Google Profile Actually Does for Your Hotel

Beyond rankings and trust, a well-reviewed Google Business Profile works as a 24/7 salesperson for your property. Here is what it unlocks:

  • Google Maps prominence: You appear higher when travellers search for hotels near your location, airport, or landmark.
  • Rich snippets in search: Your star rating displays directly in Google Search results, increasing click-through rates before guests even visit your website.
  • Voice search visibility: When someone asks Google Assistant "find me a hotel near Pune railway station," the results are heavily influenced by your Google review count and rating.
  • Competitive separation: In a market where many hotels have similar pricing and amenities, the property with 300 Google reviews at 4.6 stars will always win over one with 40 reviews at 4.2 stars.
  • Review content as free marketing: When guests mention specific positives — your rooftop breakfast, your friendly staff named Priya, your quiet rooms — that content builds credibility that no advertisement can replicate.

A Practical Week-by-Week Plan to Build Google Reviews

You do not need a massive budget or a dedicated marketing team to build Google reviews consistently. Here is a straightforward plan any hotel owner or manager can implement:

Week 1 — Audit and set up: Check your current Google Business Profile. Ensure all information is accurate — address, phone number, check-in times, photos, amenities. Set up a direct Google review link using Google's review link generator and shorten it for easy sharing.

Week 2 — Train your front desk: Brief all front office staff on how to verbally mention leaving a Google review during check-out. Keep it simple: "If you enjoyed your stay, a quick Google review would mean a lot to us." Practice makes it feel natural.

Week 3 — Automate WhatsApp follow-ups: This is where the biggest gains come from. Set up an automated WhatsApp message that goes out 2–3 hours post check-out with a warm thank-you note and a direct Google review link. Tools like ReviewBoostPRO integrate this directly into your workflow without requiring your staff to manually send messages.

Week 4 onward — Respond to every review: Make it a daily habit to respond to new Google reviews — positive and negative. Thank happy guests by name. Address complaints professionally and constructively. Google's algorithm rewards active engagement, and potential guests read your responses just as carefully as the reviews themselves.

✅ Pro Tip Place a small, elegant card at your reception desk and inside guest rooms with a QR code that links directly to your Google review page. Many guests are happy to scan and leave a review while they are still on your property, especially after a positive interaction with your team.

The Long-Term Compounding Effect

Google reviews compound over time in a way that OTA ratings simply cannot. Every new review increases your credibility signal. Every response you write adds fresh, indexed content to your profile. Every photo a guest uploads adds visual authenticity. Over six to twelve months of consistent effort, a hotel that starts with 40 Google reviews can realistically build to 300–400 — and the impact on direct bookings and local search ranking is exponential, not linear.

Hotels in competitive markets like South Mumbai, Koramangala in Bangalore, or Banjara Hills in Hyderabad that have invested in building their Google review base are now seeing 25–35% of their bookings come through direct channels. That is a transformation in the economics of running a hotel — achieved not through expensive advertising but through the simple, disciplined act of asking happy guests to share their experience on Google.

Your OTA presence matters. Maintain it. But do not let it be the only place where your reputation lives. Build your Google presence with the same energy, and you will gradually reduce your dependence on platforms that take a significant cut of every booking you earn.

The guests who stay with you and love the experience are your greatest marketing asset. Give them an easy, frictionless way to say so on Google — and watch what happens to your business over the next twelve months.
