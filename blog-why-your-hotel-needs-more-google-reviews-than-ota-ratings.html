Why Your Hotel Needs More Google Reviews Than OTA Ratings

PK
Priya Krishnamurthy
· August 2026 · 8 min read

Picture this: A family in Pune is planning a long weekend trip to Coorg. The father opens MakeMyTrip, browses a few properties, shortlists three options — and then does exactly what 73% of Indian travellers do next. He opens Google and searches the hotel name directly.

If your Google Business Profile shows 38 reviews and a 4.1 rating while your MakeMyTrip page proudly displays 847 reviews and a 4.6 badge, you have a problem. Not on MakeMyTrip. On Google. Because that father is going to see the 38 reviews and quietly move on to the next property.

This is the silent killer of direct bookings for Indian hotels in 2026. Hoteliers spend enormous energy chasing OTA ratings — and they should — but they are leaving an enormous amount of trust, visibility, and commission-free revenue on the table by ignoring their Google review count.

Let us break down exactly why Google reviews matter more than OTA ratings in ways most hotel owners have never considered, and what you can do about it starting today.

The First Touchpoint Nobody Talks About

OTAs are discovery engines. Travellers browse them the way people scroll Instagram — for inspiration and comparison. But the moment someone gets serious about a property, the first thing they do is Google it. This is true in Mumbai, Jaipur, Guwahati, and everywhere in between.

Google's search results show your hotel's star rating and review count directly in the search snippet, in Google Maps, and in the Knowledge Panel on the right side of the screen. Before a potential guest even clicks your website or your OTA listing, they have already formed a first impression based on two numbers: your star rating and your total review count.

⚠️ The Trust Gap A hotel with 4.7 stars on Booking.com but only 29 Google reviews looks suspicious to a savvy traveller. They wonder: "Why are people not talking about this place publicly?" Even if your OTA rating is excellent, a thin Google profile signals low credibility in the open internet era.

OTA reviews live inside a walled garden. They are only visible to people who are already on that platform. Google reviews, on the other hand, are visible everywhere — in search results, Google Maps, voice search responses, and even when someone asks Google Assistant "best boutique hotel near Rishikesh." Your OTA rating cannot help you there. Only Google can.

OTA Ratings Cost You Money. Google Reviews Do Not.

Here is the economics every hotel owner needs to sit with. Every booking that comes through MakeMyTrip, Goibibo, Booking.com, or Agoda costs you somewhere between 15% and 25% in commission. On a ₹5,000 per night room, that is ₹750 to ₹1,250 walking out of your door — per booking, per night.

Now consider what happens when a guest finds you on Google, calls your front desk directly or books through your own website. That commission stays with you. Over a year, for a 20-room property running at 60% occupancy, the difference can easily be ₹15 to ₹25 lakhs in annual savings.

Google reviews are the engine that powers direct bookings. The more reviews you have, the higher you rank on Google Maps. The higher you rank on Google Maps, the more people find your hotel without going through an OTA. More direct bookings mean more money in your pocket, not the OTA's.

Google Maps Ranking: The SEO Nobody Told You About

Most hotel owners think of Google reviews as a reputation tool. They are right — but that is only half the picture. Google reviews are also a local SEO ranking factor. Google's algorithm for Maps rankings heavily weighs three things: relevance, distance, and prominence. Your review count and recency directly impact your prominence score.

Consider two heritage havelis in Jodhpur. Both are beautiful, both charge similar rates. Hotel A has 310 Google reviews with fresh ones coming in every week. Hotel B has 85 reviews and the last one was posted four months ago. When a traveller searches "heritage hotel in Jodhpur" on Google Maps, Hotel A will almost always appear higher — sometimes dramatically so.

✅ What Google Actually Looks For
  • Review quantity: More reviews signal an active, popular business
  • Review recency: Reviews from the last 30–90 days carry more weight
  • Review diversity: Reviews mentioning different services (restaurant, spa, view) help with keyword relevance
  • Owner responses: Responding to reviews signals engagement to Google's algorithm

Your OTA rating does absolutely nothing for your Google Maps position. You could be the top-rated property on Yatra and still appear fifth on Google Maps because you have neglected your review profile. That fifth position means fewer calls, fewer direct bookings, and more dependency on OTAs to survive.

The Psychology of the Indian Traveller in 2026

Indian consumers — especially the growing middle class travelling domestically — have become remarkably sophisticated about online research. A family planning a trip to Shimla or a couple booking a resort near Goa for their anniversary will read reviews carefully. They are looking for specific signals.

On OTA platforms, travellers understand that reviews are filtered through the platform's own system. There is an implicit awareness that OTAs have business relationships with hotels, that some reviews might be gamed, and that the rating scale might be inflated. Google reviews, because they are open and public and tied to real Google accounts, feel more authentic and trustworthy to most people.

"When we were choosing between three resorts near Mahabaleshwar, all had good MakeMyTrip ratings. But one of them had 400 Google reviews with people sharing actual photos and stories. That made the decision for us. We booked directly through their website." — Ananya Sharma, traveller from Bengaluru

This is not an isolated behaviour. It is a pattern playing out across India every single day. The traveller who finds you on an OTA and then verifies you on Google is your most valuable potential guest. If your Google profile underwhelms them, they book somewhere else — or they book you through the OTA, costing you commission that should have stayed in your business.

Why Most Hotels Fail to Collect Google Reviews

Here is the irony: most hotel guests are willing to leave a review. Studies consistently show that happy customers will review when asked correctly and at the right moment. The problem is that Indian hotels almost never ask — and when they do, they ask awkwardly, at the wrong time, in the wrong channel.

A printed card on the checkout desk asking guests to "please review us online" gets ignored. An email sent three days after checkout goes to the spam folder or is forgotten. A verbal request from a tired front desk staff member at 6 AM checkout gets a polite smile and nothing else.

The right channel in India in 2026 is WhatsApp. That is where people actually read messages and take action. Hotels that have switched to sending a personalised WhatsApp message — timed perfectly at checkout or one hour after a guest leaves — are seeing review conversion rates of 30% to 50%, compared to under 5% for email or in-person requests.

OTA Reviews and Google Reviews: Stop Choosing, Do Both

To be very clear: this is not an argument to abandon your OTA review strategy. Your MakeMyTrip rating matters. Your Booking.com score matters. These platforms are still where millions of Indian travellers discover new properties every day, and a strong OTA rating gives them the initial confidence to consider you.

The point is that OTA reviews alone are not enough. They are the top of the funnel. Google reviews are what closes the deal — or loses it. A hotel that treats both as equally important will almost always outperform a hotel that focuses exclusively on one.

The practical challenge is that OTAs automatically prompt guests for reviews after checkout. Google does not. That is why your OTA review count grows naturally while your Google count stagnates. You have to actively build your Google presence with a system, not hope.
