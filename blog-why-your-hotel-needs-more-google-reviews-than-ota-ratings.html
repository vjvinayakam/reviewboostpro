The OTA Trap: You Are Building Someone Else's Reputation

MakeMyTrip, Goibibo, Agoda, Booking.com — these platforms are brilliant at pulling in guests you would never have found on your own. No sensible hotel owner should abandon them entirely. But here is the problem: every five-star review a guest leaves on an OTA platform is a review that lives inside that platform's walls. It builds their credibility, their SEO, their stickiness — not yours.

When a guest from Hyderabad types "boutique hotel in Goa" into Google, they do not see your MakeMyTrip reviews. Google shows them your Google Business Profile. And if that profile looks thin — fewer than 50 reviews, no recent activity, a 3.9 average — you have already lost to the competitor two streets away who has been quietly collecting Google reviews for the past eighteen months.

⚠️ The Hidden Cost of OTA Dependency Every booking that comes through MakeMyTrip or Booking.com costs you between 12% and 22% in commission. A ₹4,500/night room booked through an OTA costs you ₹540–₹990 per night in fees alone. A guest who finds you through Google and books directly? You keep every rupee.

How Google Reviews Directly Impact Your Revenue

Let us be very specific about how a stronger Google presence translates into money for your hotel — because this is not about vanity metrics. This is about your bottom line.

Direct bookings go up. When travellers find your hotel on Google Maps or Google Search and see 200 genuine, detailed reviews averaging 4.6 stars, they feel safe calling your front desk directly or filling your own booking form. They skip the OTA entirely. You save the commission.

You rank higher locally. Google's local algorithm treats review count, recency, and rating as three of its most important signals. A hotel in Jaipur's old city with 340 fresh Google reviews will consistently outrank a competitor with 800 MakeMyTrip ratings but only 60 Google reviews — even if the competitor has better facilities.

You attract the right guests. OTA platforms funnel everyone — price hunters, last-minute bookers, people searching for the cheapest bed. Your own Google profile attracts guests who specifically searched for you or your location. These guests spend more, complain less, and are more likely to become repeat visitors.

Why Indian Hotel Guests Rarely Leave Google Reviews (Without a Nudge)

Here is something every hotelier in India knows but rarely says out loud: Indian guests are exceptionally generous with compliments at checkout — and almost completely silent on Google afterwards. A family from Pune will tell your staff "absolutely wonderful, we will definitely come back" and then never open the Google review link.

This is not laziness or ingratitude. It is simply human behaviour. Leaving a review requires intention, and intention fades quickly once guests are back in their car heading to the airport. The window to capture that goodwill is very short — roughly 24 to 48 hours after checkout.

OTA Reviews vs Google Reviews: Understanding the Key Differences

It is worth being precise about what makes these two types of reviews fundamentally different — because many hotel owners treat them as interchangeable when they absolutely are not.

Ownership and portability. Your Google reviews belong to your Google Business Profile. They appear when anyone searches your hotel name, your address, or "hotels near [your location]." OTA reviews disappear the moment you reduce your listing or a platform changes its algorithm.

Search engine visibility. Google reviews feed directly into Google Search and Google Maps rankings. MakeMyTrip reviews have zero impact on how Google ranks your hotel. None. This single fact should shift your entire review strategy.

Trust signals for different audiences. OTA reviews are trusted by bargain-hunters comparing five similar properties. Google reviews are trusted by everyone — including corporate travel bookers, wedding planners, and group organisers who will never use an OTA but will absolutely check your Google rating before sending an inquiry.

Long-term compounding. A hotel in Udaipur that collects 20 new Google reviews every month will have 480 additional reviews by the end of two years. Each review makes the next one more trustworthy. OTA reviews, once a guest has left the platform, rarely get seen again.

A Practical System for Collecting Google Reviews at Your Hotel

Knowing that Google reviews matter is not enough. You need a repeatable system that generates reviews consistently — without putting extra pressure on your already-busy staff. Here is what works for Indian hotels specifically.

Step 1: Collect WhatsApp numbers at check-in. This is already standard practice at most Indian properties for sending bills and updates. Make sure your front desk team notes the number with explicit permission to follow up post-stay.

Step 2: Automate the ask. Configure your post-checkout message to go out automatically 2–4 hours after the guest leaves. The message should be warm, specific, and brief. Something like: "Namaste Ritu ji, thank you for staying with us at The Saffron House! We hope you enjoyed your time in Jaipur. Your feedback means everything to us — would you take 60 seconds to share your experience on Google?" Then include the direct link.

Step 3: Make it one tap. Never send guests to your hotel website and ask them to find the reviews tab. Give them a direct link to your Google review form. Reducing friction is the single biggest thing you can do to improve your conversion rate from message to review.

Step 4: Respond to every review. Google rewards hotels that respond to reviews with slightly better local rankings. More importantly, when a potential guest from Chennai reads your reviews and sees that you personally respond to every piece of feedback — positive or negative — it builds enormous trust. This is a differentiator very few Indian hotels are using well.

Step 5: Track and improve. Set a monthly target. If you have 60 rooms and average 70% occupancy, you are hosting roughly 1,400–1,600 guest parties per month. Even a 10% review conversion rate should give you 140–160 new Google reviews monthly. If you are getting far fewer, your ask is either too late, too generic, or too complicated.
