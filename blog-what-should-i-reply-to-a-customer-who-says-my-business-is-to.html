What Should I Reply to a Customer Who Says My Business Is Too Expensive?

RP
ReviewBoostPRO Team
• August 2026 • 8 min read

Picture this: You run a thriving dental clinic in South Mumbai, an upscale salon in Bangalore's Indiranagar, or a trusted auto repair workshop in Pune. You've spent years perfecting your craft, sourcing top-tier materials, hiring skilled staff, and maintaining pristine hygiene standards. Then, a prospective customer walks in, looks at your menu board or quotation slip, shakes their head, and drops the dreaded sentence: "Arey, your prices are too high! The shop down the street is doing it for half."

For Indian business owners, hearing that your service or product is "too expensive" is enough to trigger an instant wave of defensive frustration. In a hyper-competitive market like ours—where bargaining is practically a cultural sport—price objections are an everyday reality. Whether it is a patient questioning a root canal fee of ₹8,000, a diner balking at a ₹450 paneer tikka, or a car owner complaining about a ₹3,000 servicing bill, how you respond in that exact split-second determines whether you lose a sale or gain a lifelong advocate.

Worse yet is when these frustrations spill online into Google Business Profile reviews. A stinging 1-star review complaining that your business is a "lootmaar" (daylight robbery) can instantly deter hundreds of prospective clients scrolling through maps on their phones. In this guide, we will break down the psychology of Indian consumers when it comes to pricing, explore exact scripts for face-to-face and online price complaints, and show you how to safeguard your local brand reputation.

Understanding the Indian Consumer's "Value vs. Price" Mindset

When an Indian customer says your business is expensive, they rarely mean they literally cannot afford it. More often than not, they are telling you that they do not yet see the value that justifies your price point. In our culture of value-seeking, consumers want *paisa vasool* (complete value for money). If they see two dental clinics or two salons side by side, their default assumption is that the service is identical, so the cheaper one must be the smarter choice.

Your job as a business owner is to bridge that cognitive gap immediately. You must shift the conversation away from raw numbers and redirect it toward quality, safety, durability, and peace of mind. When a customer in Delhi complains about your plumbing or salon rates, reacting defensively by saying, *"Go to the cheap roadside place then!"* will only alienate them and guarantee a negative online review later.

The Danger of Discounting Never immediately slash your prices when someone complains. Dropping your rates signals to the customer that your original pricing was arbitrary or inflated, which destroys your perceived authority and brand trust instantly.

How to Reply Face-to-Face or Over WhatsApp

When a price objection happens live in your store, clinic, or over a WhatsApp business chat, your reply needs to be calm, empathetic, and anchored in the tangible benefits you provide. Here are three effective frameworks you can use:

  • The Quality & Safety Anchor: Acknowledge their concern while subtly reminding them of what sets you apart. Example: "Sir, I completely understand that our package is ₹500 more than the market average. That is because we use 100% genuine OEM spare parts with a 1-year warranty, whereas cheaper shops use local refurbished parts that fail within months."
  • The Long-Term Cost Reversal: Reframe the expense as a money-saver over time. Example for clinics or auto repair shops: "Ma'am, doing this procedure with sub-standard materials might save you ₹2,000 today, but it often leads to complications that cost three times more to fix down the line. We do it right the very first time."
  • The Breakdown Technique: Transparency builds instant trust. Walk them through exactly where the money goes—certified professionals, premium imported products, sanitization protocols, and dedicated post-service support.

Handling Price Complaints on Google Reviews

What happens when a disgruntled price-complainer doesn't buy from you, but instead logs onto Google Maps to leave a 1-star review saying your prices are exorbitant? These public reviews are dangerous because future customers read them without context. You cannot delete them, but you can neutralize them with a masterful public response.

Your response to a negative price review on Google should never be aggressive. It is not written for the angry reviewer—it is written for the hundred prospective customers who will read it tomorrow. Keep it professional, polite, and focused on value.

Sample Public Reply Template "Hello [Customer Name], thank you for sharing your feedback. At [Business Name], we pride ourselves on using top-grade materials, certified specialists, and maintaining strict safety standards that ensure lasting results for our clients in [City]. While our rates reflect these high standards and comprehensive warranties, we regret if our pricing did not meet your expectations. We wish you the very best!"

By posting a response like this, you show every future reader that your business is confident in its quality, professional, and composed under pressure. It completely neutralizes the sting of a pricing complaint.

Proactive Strategies to Prevent Price Resistance

The best way to handle price objections is to make sure they happen less often in the first place. Local businesses across Hyderabad, Mumbai, and Kolkata can implement these three proactive steps today:

  • Highlight Social Proof Early: When customers see that hundreds of people happily pay your prices and rave about the results, price resistance drops naturally. Display your ratings proudly in your waiting area and on your digital touchpoints.
  • Bundle Value Add-ons: Instead of lowering your core price, add minor complimentary perks—such as a free follow-up checkup, complimentary home delivery, or a loyalty discount voucher—that make the main price feel entirely justified.
  • Automate Review Collection: Most happy customers forget to leave reviews, while unhappy bargain-hunters are lightning-fast to complain.
