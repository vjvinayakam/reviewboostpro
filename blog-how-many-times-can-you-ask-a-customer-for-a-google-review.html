How Many Times Can You Ask a Customer for a Google Review?

RB
ReviewBoostPRO Editorial Team
• August 14, 2026 • 6 min read

Picture this: Rajesh walks into a popular dental clinic in Bandra, Mumbai, for a root canal procedure. The treatment goes smoothly, the dentist is courteous, and the bill comes to ₹4,500. As Rajesh steps out, he receives a polite WhatsApp message asking for feedback and a Google review. He is busy driving back to his office, so he ignores it.

The next day, another text reminder pings his phone. He's in a morning meeting. He ignores it again. By the fourth ping in three days, Rajesh feels annoyed. Instead of leaving a 5-star review, he ignores the clinic altogether or, worse, drops a negative rating because he feels harassed.

For local business owners across India—whether you run a trendy salon in Koramangala, Bangalore, a busy family restaurant in Connaught Place, New Delhi, or an auto repair shop in Pune—collecting Google reviews is essential for local SEO. But it raises a delicate question: How many times can you ask a customer for a review before it crosses the line into spam?

The Short Answer: The Golden Rule of Review Frequency

The short, definitive answer is maximum 2 to 3 times total. Ideally, you should send an initial request immediately after the service is rendered, followed by one single, well-timed reminder 48 to 72 hours later if they haven't responded.

Anything beyond three requests runs a high risk of annoying your patrons, damaging your brand reputation, and violating the unspoken etiquette of customer communication in India, where WhatsApp inboxes are already crowded with promotional messages from banks, e-commerce giants, and delivery apps.

⚠️ The Danger of Over-Asking When local businesses send 4, 5, or even 6 automated pings asking for a review, conversion rates plummet. Worse, customers flag your WhatsApp business account for spam, which can lead to temporary numbers bans from Meta. Keep your outreach disciplined and respectful.

Why Timing Matters More Than Quantity

In the Indian market, context and timing dictate whether a customer will tap your Google review link or swipe away your message. A message sent while the service experience is still fresh in their mind converts exponentially better than one sent a week later.

Let's look at how different local businesses should space out their requests:

  • Restaurants & Cafes: Send the review link via WhatsApp while the customer is still at the table paying their bill, or within 2 hours of departure while they are digesting their meal. Reminder? None needed for casual dining. If it's fine dining or home delivery, one gentle follow-up the next afternoon works well.
  • Salons & Spas: Send the initial request as they walk out the salon doors in Hyderabad or Chennai. Follow up exactly once, 3 days later, when their fresh haircut or facial is looking great and drawing compliments.
  • Clinics & Pharmacies: Send the request once the patient reaches home after picking up their prescription. Follow up 48 hours later asking how they are feeling, coupled with a gentle nudge to share their feedback on Google to help other local patients.
  • Auto Repair & Service Centers: Send the review link the moment the vehicle is delivered back to the owner. Follow up once after 5 to 7 days, giving them enough time to drive the vehicle and test the repairs.

Crafting the Perfect 2-Step WhatsApp Sequence

Because Indian consumers heavily rely on WhatsApp for daily communication, it is by far the highest-converting channel for review generation. However, your message copy must feel personal, warm, and frictionless.

Here is a proven 2-step sequence that respects the customer's time while maximizing your review count:

Message 1 (Immediate - Sent right after billing/service):
"Namaste Rajesh ji! Thank you for visiting Dr. Sharma’s Dental Clinic in Mumbai today. We hope you are feeling comfortable. Could you take 30 seconds to share your experience with us on Google? It helps our small team immensely: [Short Google Review Link]"

Message 2 (The One & Only Reminder - 48 hours later):
"Hi Rajesh ji, just checking in to see how your tooth is feeling after your visit with us on Tuesday. If you have a quick moment today, we’d love your feedback on Google here: [Short Google Review Link]. Have a wonderful week!"

Notice how polite, respectful, and non-pushy this approach is. It acknowledges the customer by name, asks about their well-being, and provides a direct link without demanding compliance.

💡 Pro-Tip for Local Business Owners Never incentivize reviews with cash or heavy discounts in exchange for 5 stars—Google's algorithms can detect review manipulation and penalize your Map listing. Instead, focus on asking at the absolute peak of customer delight, using a frictionless WhatsApp link.

Summary: Your Action Plan

To summarize, stop spamming your customers with endless reminder loops. Stick to these core rules:

  • Cap your requests at a maximum of 2 touches per customer lifecycle.
  • Space your requests out intelligently (Instant + 48 to 72 hours later).
  • Use WhatsApp as your primary channel for Indian consumers.
  • Automate your workflow so you never accidentally double-message someone.

When you respect your customers' time and attention, they will happily reward your business with glowing 5-star reviews that push you to the top of Google Local Pack rankings in your city.
