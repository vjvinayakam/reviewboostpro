How Salons Should Respond to Negative Google Reviews

RB
ReviewBoostPRO Editorial Team
• August 2026 • 8 min read

Picture this: It is a busy Saturday evening at your salon in Bandra, Mumbai or Indiranagar, Bangalore. Your stylists are rushing from styling stations to shampoo bowls, bookings are fully packed, and clients are waiting in your lounge. Then, your phone buzzes with a notification. A new Google review has landed. You open it up, and your heart sinks—a 1-star rating with a scathing review claiming a hair color went entirely wrong or a bridal makeup session was botched.

For any salon owner or manager, seeing a negative review feels deeply personal. Beauty and grooming services are intimate. When someone criticizes your work online, it feels like an attack on your creative talent, your staff, and your livelihood. In India's hyper-competitive beauty market, where neighborhood salons compete fiercely with high-end luxury chains and tech-enabled home salon aggregators, a single harsh review can cause potential clients walking past your Google Maps listing to immediately click away.

However, panic is your worst enemy. How you respond to a negative Google review matters far more to prospective clients than the review itself. In fact, a professional, empathetic, and prompt response can transform an angry critic into a loyal, returning customer, while signaling to hundreds of onlookers that your salon values accountability and top-tier service.

Why Negative Reviews Hit Salons Harder Than Other Businesses

Unlike an auto repair shop or a pharmacy where transactions are purely functional, a salon visit is an emotional, confidence-boosting experience. When a client books a global hair coloring service worth ₹6,000 or a bridal package costing ₹25,000, they have sky-high expectations. If the shade is slightly off or the blow-dry falls flat within an hour of stepping out into the humid Delhi or Chennai weather, disappointment quickly turns into public anger.

Furthermore, beauty services are visual. A negative comment accompanied by photos of a patchy haircut or uneven threading can severely damage your brand image. Local customers looking for a trustworthy stylist rely heavily on reviews. If they see unresolved complaints, they assume your staff lacks skill or your management lacks empathy.

The Danger of Defensive Replies Never argue back, dispute facts aggressively, or blame the customer publicly. Saying things like "You didn't explain what you wanted properly" or "Our prices are clearly listed" makes your salon look arrogant and unprofessional to everyone else reading the thread.

Step 1: Stay Calm and Act Fast (Within 24 Hours)

The golden rule of online reputation management is speed. Try to respond to any negative review within 24 hours. A prompt reply shows potential clients that your salon is active, attentive, and deeply invested in customer satisfaction. When you see a bad review, step away for five minutes to let your initial emotional spike settle. Do not type a reply while your blood is boiling.

Remember that your response is not actually meant to win an argument with the disgruntled reviewer—it is a public performance for the 500 future clients who will read your exchange next week. They want to see how calm, polite, and solution-oriented your management team is.

Step 2: Use the LAER Formula (Listen, Acknowledge, Empathize, Resolve)

Crafting a great response requires structure. The best framework for service businesses is the LAER approach:

  • Listen & Acknowledge: Thank the reviewer for taking the time to share their feedback. Acknowledge the specific service they booked (e.g., keratin treatment, balayage, or facial).
  • Empathize: Validate their frustration. If they felt rushed or unhappy with the outcome, apologize that their experience did not meet the high standards you strive for.
  • Take It Offline: Never negotiate refunds or corrective appointments in a public comment thread. Provide a direct phone number or email address to continue the conversation privately.
Example of a Perfect Salon Response "Hi Priya, thank you for sharing your feedback. We are so sorry to hear that your hair coloring session at our Koramangala branch didn't meet your expectations. We pride ourselves on perfection, and it's clear we fell short during your visit. Please reach out directly to our salon manager, Amit, at +91-98765-XXXXX so we can invite you back for a complimentary corrective treatment and consultation. We’d love the chance to make this right!"

Step 3: Move the Conversation to WhatsApp

Resolving a dispute is infinitely easier on a direct, personal messaging channel like WhatsApp than through awkward phone calls or dragged-out email chains. In India, WhatsApp is how business gets done. Once the client calls or messages your management number, handle them with supreme care. Offer them a complimentary touch-up, a complimentary deep conditioning spa, or a sincere remedy.

Once you have successfully resolved the issue offline and the client is happy with your corrective service, kindly ask them if they would be open to updating their Google review. Most clients, touched by your genuine willingness to fix the problem, will gladly upgrade their 1-star review to a glowing 4 or 5-star review!

The easiest way to deal with occasional negative reviews is to ensure they are heavily outnumbered by authentic, 5-star reviews from your happy regular clients. Most happy customers walk out of your salon feeling fabulous, but they simply forget to leave a review on Google because life gets busy.

This is where automation changes everything. Instead of front desk receptionists awkwardly asking clients for Google reviews while billing, smart salon owners use tools like ReviewBoostPRO. By automatically sending a polite WhatsApp message with a direct review link right after the client steps out of the salon chair, you make leaving feedback effortless.

How Automation Protects Your Salon Rating When your salon collects 40 to 60 new 5-star reviews every single month via automated WhatsApp prompts, a stray 1-star review from an unreasonable customer will have negligible impact on your overall 4.8-star local rating.

Step 5: Train Your Floor Staff to Catch Issues Before They Hit Google

Prevention is always better than cure. Many negative reviews happen because clients are too polite to complain to the stylist on the spot, but vent their frustrations online once they get home. Train your senior stylists and front-desk managers to run a simple feedback check before the client pays their bill.

Ask simple questions while the client is looking in the mirror: "How do you feel about the length?" or "Are you happy with how the layers fell?" If a client hesitates or expresses slight dissatisfaction, fix it immediately in the salon chair. A client who gets a free adjustment on the spot leaves happy and will never write a scathing review online.
