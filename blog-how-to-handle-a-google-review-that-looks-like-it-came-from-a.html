How to Handle a Google Review That Looks Like It Came From a Competitor

RB
ReviewBoostPRO Editorial Team
• August 14, 2026 • 8 min read

Imagine waking up in Mumbai, opening your Google Business Profile with your morning chai, and seeing a devastating 1-star review. The reviewer claims your dental clinic, restaurant, or salon gave them an abysmal experience, charged exorbitant prices like ₹5,000 for basic services, and claims "the doctor was rude" or "the food was completely cold."

You search your booking system, billing software, and appointment logs for the name. Nothing. You check your CCTV footage. No such customer ever walked through your doors in Andheri or Bandra. You realize with a sinking feeling: this isn't a dissatisfied customer. This is economic sabotage. This review came straight from a competitor.

In India’s hyper-competitive local markets—from bustling medical corridors in Delhi to crowded retail hubs in Bangalore and Pune—dirty tactics happen. Unscrupulous rivals sometimes resort to fake negative reviews to sink your local search rankings and swipe your prospective customers.

Panicking and posting an angry, defensive reply is the worst thing you can do. Let’s look at how to systematically spot, report, and neutralize competitor sabotage without losing your cool.

Step 1: Verify Your Suspicions (Are You Sure It's a Competitor?)

Before launching an investigation or flagging the review, make sure you have solid grounds to suspect foul play. Genuine unhappy customers do exist, and treating a real complaint like a fake review can destroy your brand reputation.

Look for these telltale signs of competitor sabotage:

  • Vague, Generic Complaints: The review uses sweeping negative statements ("Worst service ever," "Terrible quality") without mentioning specific staff names, exact treatments, or specific dishes ordered.
  • Anonymous or Suspicious Profiles: The reviewer has a blank profile picture, a strange randomized username, and has only ever left 1-star reviews for businesses in the exact same industry across your city.
  • The Timing Factor: The review drops immediately after you launched a new promotional discount, opened a new branch, or outranked a local rival in Google Maps search results.
  • Promotion of Alternatives: In extreme cases, the reviewer’s text or replies subtly hint at visiting another nearby establishment instead.
Important Reality Check Never accuse a competitor by name in your public response. Keep your suspicions strictly internal. Your public reply is meant for potential customers reading your profile, not for fighting personal vendettas.

Step 2: Draft a Strategic Public Reply (For Future Customers)

Google takes time to evaluate removal requests. While your removal appeal is pending, hundreds of potential customers will see that 1-star review. Your public response should not be an emotional outburst; it must be a professional shield.

Your goal is to signal to anyone reading that this review is either fake or an isolated misunderstanding, while showcasing your professional maturity.

Use a template similar to this:

"Hello [Name], we take all feedback very seriously at our clinic, but we have searched our patient records for any transaction or appointment matching your details and cannot find any record of your visit. We pride ourselves on transparent, top-tier service. Please call our clinic manager directly at [Phone Number] so we can identify your account and resolve any genuine issues immediately." — Standard Professional Sabotage Response Template

If it is a fake review from a rival, they will never call the number. Prospective customers reading this will immediately notice that you handled the accusation with grace and professionalism, while the reviewer vanished.

Step 3: Flag and Report the Review to Google

Google has strict guidelines against fake reviews, conflict-of-interest ratings, and spam. Competitor reviews violate Google’s policy under "Misrepresentation" and "Conflict of Interest."

Here is how to properly escalate the review to Google:

  • Log into your Google Business Profile dashboard.
  • Navigate to the "Reviews" tab and locate the suspicious comment.
  • Click the three vertical dots next to the review and select "Report review".
  • Choose the appropriate violation category—usually "Conflict of Interest" (if you know a competitor posted it) or "Spam".

If Google’s automated system initially rejects your removal request, don't despair. You can escalate the ticket via the Google Business Profile Help Community forum, where product experts review escalated cases of malicious sabotage.

Waiting for Google to remove a malicious review can take days or even weeks. In the meantime, that single negative rating can drag down your overall star rating and hurt your local SEO visibility.

Step 5: Strengthen Your Digital Fortifications

Once you survive a competitor review attack, take proactive steps to ensure your Google listing remains resilient against future attempts.

  • Build a Buffer: A business with 150+ reviews can absorb a random 1-star review without its average rating dipping below 4.7 stars. Keep a steady stream of incoming reviews flowing consistently.
  • Monitor Daily: Set up Google Business Profile notifications so you are alerted the exact minute a new review drops. Quick response times minimize damage.
  • Maintain Records: Keep detailed appointment registers, invoices, and digital logs so you can quickly prove to Google Support that a reviewer was never your customer.
