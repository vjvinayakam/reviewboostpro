What Is the Best Time to Ask a Customer for a Google Review?

RB
ReviewBoostPRO Team
• August 2026 • 7 min read

If you run a local business in India—whether it is a dental clinic in South Mumbai, a busy family restaurant in Bangalore, a premium salon in Delhi, or a trusted auto repair shop in Chennai—you already know how vital Google reviews are. When a prospective customer searches for services nearby, your Google Business Profile rating can make or break their decision to walk through your doors or scroll past.

However, getting customers to leave a review feels like an uphill battle for many Indian business owners. You might have tried handing out printed QR code cards at the billing counter or dropping a polite SMS request on an outdated billing software. The result? Crickets. Zero responses, or worse, forgotten intentions.

The secret to skyrocketing your review collection rate isn't just about what you say in your request—it is almost entirely about when you ask. Timing dictates momentum. Ask too early, and the customer hasn't experienced the full value of your service. Ask too late, and the emotional high of a great experience has completely faded away.

Let's dive deep into the science of timing and discover the absolute best moments to ask your customers for a glowing Google review.

The Golden Rule: Strike While the Emotion is Fresh

Human psychology dictates that people are most willing to help and praise a business when they are experiencing peak satisfaction. For local businesses, this peak occurs at the exact moment a pain point is successfully resolved or when a pleasurable experience concludes.

Consider Rajesh, who runs a multi-brand car service workshop in Pune. For months, his team asked customers to review them via an email sent two days after vehicle delivery. The conversion rate hovered miserably around 2%. Why? Because two days later, the car had already been driven through Mumbai-Pune highway dust, and the excitement of a smooth service was buried under office emails and daily chores.

When Rajesh shifted his strategy to send a personalized WhatsApp message exactly 45 minutes after the customer drove out of the workshop—while they were still enjoying the clean interior and smooth engine pickup—his review generation shot up by 300%.

Key Takeaway The ideal window for a positive response is within 1 to 3 hours of service completion. Once 24 hours pass, your chances of getting that review drop by more than 70%.

Industry-Specific Best Times to Ask

Every Indian business sector has a unique customer journey. What works for a pharmacy in Hyderabad will not necessarily work for a luxury hotel in Jaipur. Let's break down the optimal timing across popular local categories:

  • Clinics & Healthcare (Dental, Pediatric, General): Send the review request 2 to 3 hours after the appointment concludes. By then, local anesthesia has worn off, the patient is resting comfortably at home, and they deeply appreciate the care and pain-free treatment they received.
  • Restaurants & Cafes: The best time is right as they pay their bill or immediately after they step out of the restaurant. If you use a digital billing system, a friendly WhatsApp prompt sent as they walk to their car or book an auto ensures high engagement while the taste of the Biryani or espresso is still fresh.
  • Salons & Spas: Right after the final hair blow-dry or massage, when the customer looks in the mirror and loves their transformation. Handing them your direct review link via WhatsApp while they settle their bill at the reception desk works like magic.
  • Auto Repair Shops & Garages: Exactly when the customer arrives to pick up their vehicle and makes the final payment of ₹5,000 or ₹15,000, or within 1 hour of driving back home.
  • Pharmacies & Medical Stores: Send a quick WhatsApp check-in the next morning to ask if the prescribed medicines are helping, coupled with a polite request to rate your neighborhood chemist shop.

Why WhatsApp is the Ultimate Channel for Indian Consumers

Timing your ask perfectly is only half the battle; the delivery channel matters just as much. In India, SMS inboxes are flooded with promotional spam, bank OTPs, and courier alerts where real messages get lost. Emails are frequently ignored or end up in the spam folder.

WhatsApp, on the other hand, is checked over 50 times a day by the average Indian smartphone user. It feels personal, immediate, and trustworthy. When your automated system sends a friendly, customized WhatsApp message at the exact right hour, it feels like a natural extension of great customer service.

When You Should NEVER Ask for a Review

Knowing when *not* to ask is just as crucial as knowing the right time. Bad timing can frustrate customers and invite negative or retaliatory reviews. Avoid asking for reviews in the following scenarios:

  • During an active service dispute or billing confusion: If a customer is arguing about a delayed order or an unexpected charge at your counter, resolve it first. Never trigger an automated review sequence until the customer is completely satisfied.
  • Late night hours (After 9:00 PM or before 8:00 AM): Pinging a customer's WhatsApp late at night is intrusive and unprofessional. Respect their personal time.
  • When the service outcome was subpar: If a patient had to wait 2 hours past their appointment time or a dish was served cold, skip the automated review trigger for that specific transaction.
