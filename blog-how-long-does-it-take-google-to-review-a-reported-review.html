How Long Does It Take Google to Review a Reported Review?

RB
ReviewBoostPRO Editorial Team
• August 14, 2026 • 7 min read

Picture this scenario: You open your Google Business Profile dashboard on a hectic Tuesday morning at your clinic in Bandra, Mumbai, or your busy auto repair garage in Pune. Right at the top sits a glaring 1-star review from an account you’ve never serviced. The text is abusive, completely false, and threatens to hurt your local search rankings and daily walk-ins. You immediately click "Report Review", selecting "Conflict of Interest" or "Spam".

Then comes the agonizing wait. Days turn into weeks. The review remains live, mocking your business, while potential customers read it and choose your competitors instead. You wonder: Does Google even care? When will they actually look at my report?

If you are an Indian business owner grappling with spam, malicious competitor attacks, or unjustified negative ratings, understanding Google's internal review timeline is critical. Let’s break down exactly how long the process takes, what happens behind the scenes, and how you can dramatically improve your chances of getting fake reviews removed.

The Short Answer: Google's Official Timeline

According to Google's official documentation, the automated and manual review process for a reported review typically takes anywhere from 24 hours to 7 business days. However, for local businesses operating across competitive Indian markets like Delhi NCR, Bangalore, Chennai, or Hyderabad, the reality on the ground can often feel entirely different.

In practice, the timeline depends heavily on how the removal request is processed. Here is what you can realistically expect:

  • Automated Triage (Within 24-48 Hours): When you first hit report, Google’s automated algorithms scan the review for obvious policy violations, such as profanity, restricted content, or known bot patterns. If the algorithm catches a blatant violation, the review vanishes almost instantly.
  • Manual Moderation Queue (3 to 7 Days): If the automated filter doesn't catch the violation, your report is escalated to a human moderator queue. This stage routinely takes between 3 to 7 working days, and during peak periods or holidays, it can stretch past two weeks.
  • The "Never Removed" Black Hole: A significant percentage of reported reviews are simply never acted upon because Google’s policies interpret the complaint as a "disagreement over customer experience" rather than an outright violation of terms of service.
The Reality Check Google handles millions of business listings and hundreds of millions of reviews globally. Human moderation resources are severely limited, which explains why subtle fake reviews crafted by competing businesses often slip right past automated filters and linger for weeks.

How to Check the Status of Your Reported Review

Unlike customer service tickets where you receive a live tracking link, Google’s Business Profile Support offers very limited visibility into the moderation status of a reported review. Many Indian merchants get confused about where to check.

To check your reported review status, you need to use the Google Business Profile Help Management Tool (formerly the Removals Tool):

  1. Log into your Google account associated with your business profile.
  2. Navigate to the official Google Review Management portal.
  3. View the list of reviews you have previously flagged.
  4. Check the status indicator: It will typically display as "Decision pending", "Escalated", or "No policy violation found".

If the status says "No policy violation found," it means Google’s system has reviewed the report and decided the review stays up. Do not panic—you still have recourse through the GBP Help Community Forum, where Product Experts can manually escalate egregious violations.

Why Do Some Reports Take Forever (Or Get Rejected)?

If you've ever watched a malicious 1-star review sit untouched for a month, you know how frustrating it is. Google rejects or stalls removal requests for several specific reasons:

1. Mistaking Customer Disputes for Fake Reviews: Google views itself as a neutral platform. If a customer had a genuine bad experience at your Mumbai restaurant or Chennai pharmacy—even if they exaggerated or were rude—Google will not remove it. They won't arbitrate whether the chicken curry was actually too spicy or whether your clinic reception was slow.

2. Vague Reporting Categories: Simply clicking "Report" without providing context limits your success rate. Generic flags lack the precise evidence human moderators need to quickly verify that an account is a spammer or a fake profile set up by a rival.

3. High-Volume Queue Backlogs: Certain sectors in India—such as real estate agencies, multi-specialty clinics, dental practices, and trendy cafes in Bangalore—experience heavy review manipulation and spam wars, flooding Google's regional moderation queues.

Actionable Steps If Your Review Is Still Live After 7 Days

If a week has passed and your reported review is still active, take these tactical steps rather than repeatedly reporting the same comment:

  • Post a Professional Public Reply: Write a calm, factual response addressing the reviewer. Remember, future customers are reading your reply, not the angry troll. State clearly: "Hello, we have no record of any patient/customer under this name or with this issue in our database. Please contact our manager at our official number so we can verify and assist you."
  • Escalate via the Google Help Community: Create a new thread on the official Google Business Profile Help Community forum. Detail your case clearly, provide screenshots, and explain how the review violates Google’s restricted content guidelines. Silver or Gold Product Experts often review these community threads and can escalate blatant violations directly to Google engineering teams.
