What Should I Reply to a Customer Who Has Never Used My Business?

RB
ReviewBoostPRO Team
• August 24, 2026 • 7 min read

Picture this: You run a busy dental clinic in Bandra, Mumbai, or a popular North Indian restaurant in Connaught Place, New Delhi. You open your Google Business Profile dashboard with your morning chai, ready to greet your day, and you spot a brand-new 1-star review. You click on the profile, check your appointment logs, billing software, and CCTV footage. Absolutely zero record. This person has never walked through your doors, never booked a table, and certainly never paid a single rupee for your services.

For Indian local business owners, dealing with ghost reviewers or malicious competitors who leave fake negative reviews is an infuriating reality. Whether it is a disgruntled ex-employee in Bangalore, a mischievous competitor down the street in Pune, or simply a mistaken identity case in Chennai, responding incorrectly can destroy your local reputation. What you type in that public response box is not just a reply to the reviewer—it is a billboard for every future customer reading your profile.

In this guide, we will break down the psychology of phantom reviews, how to identify whether they are genuine errors or malicious attacks, and the exact word-for-word templates you can use to protect your brand integrity.

Why Do People Leave Reviews for Businesses They Never Visited?

Before firing off an angry response, it helps to understand why this happens. Not all phantom reviews are malicious; some are honest human errors, while others are calculated tactics.

  • Mistaken Identity: India has thousands of businesses with similar names. A customer in Hyderabad might intend to review "Sharma Auto Repair" in Ameerpet, but accidentally tap on your auto repair shop in Jubilee Hills.
  • Competitor Sabotage: Unethical local rivals sometimes hire bot farms or use fake profiles to drop 1-star ratings on top-performing local businesses to siphon away foot traffic.
  • Trolls and Pranksters: Bored teenagers or online trolls occasionally leave bizarre or negative comments on random local listings just for amusement.
  • Grudging Ex-Staff or Personal Vendettas: Disgruntled former employees, vendors, or personal acquaintances may target your public profile to cause distress.
⚠️ The Golden Rule of Public Responses Never sound defensive, emotional, or rude in your public Google reply. Even if the review is 100% fraudulent, prospective customers in cities like Jaipur or Ahmedabad are watching how calmly and professionally you handle adversity.

Step 1: Check Your Records Thoroughly

Before you type a single word, verify. Search your customer management software, WhatsApp chat logs, billing archives, and reservation books. Cross-check the name and profile photo. If you run a pharmacy in Kolkata or a boutique salon in Ahmedabad, check your physical registers or digital appointment books.

If you genuinely cannot find any trace of this transaction, do not panic. You have two immediate tracks of action: reporting the review to Google for violating terms of service, and crafting a public rebuttal that signals professionalism.

Step 2: File a Removal Request with Google

Google’s content policies explicitly forbid fake reviews, spam, and conflicts of interest. Click the three dots next to the review on your Google Business Profile dashboard and select "Report Review". Choose "Conflict of Interest" or "Misrepresentation". While Google's automated moderation can sometimes take days or weeks, it is a crucial first step.

However, Google does not always take down fake reviews immediately. While you wait for their review team, your active public response must fill the gap so potential clients do not assume the worst.

Step 3: Choose Your Reply Strategy Based on Tone

Your response should serve two distinct purposes: invite genuine offline resolution if it is an honest mistake, or politely expose the fact that the reviewer has no connection to your establishment if it is malicious.

Template 1: For Suspected Mistaken Identity (Polite & Helpful)

Use this when you suspect the customer meant to visit another business with a similar name in your city.

"Hello [Name], thank you for reaching out. After thoroughly checking our appointment and client records at our [City] location, we cannot find any record of you visiting or using our services. We suspect this might be a mix-up with another local business sharing a similar name. Could you please reach out to our manager directly at [Phone Number] so we can clear this up immediately?" — Best for Clinics, Salons & Spas

Template 2: For Malicious Competitor Attacks (Firm & Professional)

Use this when you are certain it is a fake review intended to harm your business rating.

"Hi [Name], we take all feedback extremely seriously, but after checking our database, we have no record of anyone under your name or details ever utilizing our services. As a verified business committed to quality service in [City], we kindly request you to contact our support desk with your invoice or transaction details so we can investigate. If no business was conducted, we request you to remove this review." — Best for Restaurants, Auto Shops & Hotels

Summary Checklist for Handling Ghost Reviewers

  • Never get into an online argument: Emotional outbursts in public review threads validate fake reviewers and turn off genuine buyers.
  • Audit your database first: Ensure you are not missing a real customer who used a different spelling or phone number.
  • Report to Google: Always flag policy-violating reviews through your business dashboard.
  • Deploy professional templates: Clearly state that no transaction record exists while keeping the door open if it was a genuine administrative error.
