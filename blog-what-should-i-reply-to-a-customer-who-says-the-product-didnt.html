What Should I Reply to a Customer Who Says the Product Didn't Work?

RB
ReviewBoostPRO Team
• August 2026 • 7 min read

Picture this scenario: You run a thriving pharmacy in South Mumbai or an ayurvedic clinic in Pune. A customer walks in—or worse, leaves a public Google review—claiming, "I bought this ₹1,200 supplement/cream, and it completely didn't work. Total waste of money!"

Your blood pressure spikes. Your immediate instinct might be to defend your product, explain the chemical formulation, or tell them they probably didn’t use it right. Pause right there. In the Indian retail and service market, reacting defensively to a performance complaint is a fast-track ticket to local reputational damage.

Whether you manage an auto repair shop in Chennai fixing complex engines or a premium salon in Bangalore offering keratin treatments, handling product and service dissatisfaction requires a delicate mix of empathy, professional boundaries, and swift communication. Let's look at exactly how to handle these tricky situations.

1. The Golden Rule: Never Argue Publicly

When a customer complains online that something didn’t work, prospective buyers aren't just reading the complaint—they are watching how you react. If you sound aggressive, dismissive, or accuse the customer of lying, future customers will walk away.

Instead, validate their frustration without admitting fault regarding product efficacy. Acknowledge that their experience fell short of expectations, and immediately take the conversation to a private channel—preferably WhatsApp.

⚠️ What NOT to Reply Publicly "Sir, you are the only one complaining out of 500 bottles sold. You clearly didn't follow the instructions printed on the back. No refunds."
✅ The Winning Public Response Script "Hello Amit, we are truly sorry to hear that the results didn't match your expectations. We want every customer in Delhi to get full value for their money. Could you please message us directly on WhatsApp at [Phone Number] or share your details so our lead expert can look into this right away?"

2. Shift the Battleground to WhatsApp Immediately

Public comment sections are terrible places to solve technical or performance grievances. You need to transition the customer to a private environment where you can ask probing questions about how they used the item, inspect proof of purchase, and offer a resolution.

In India, WhatsApp is king. Customers prefer chatting over phone calls because it feels less confrontational. Once they text you on WhatsApp, you can ask for photos, batch numbers, or service logs.

3. Investigate Without Interrogating

Once you are chatting privately, your tone must be collaborative rather than defensive. Ask open-ended, helpful questions:

  • "Could you walk me through how you applied or used it over the last few days?"
  • "Did you face any unexpected side effects or unusual reactions?"
  • "Let's check the batch number on the packaging so we can verify it with our distributor."

Often, "the product didn't work" stems from incorrect usage. For instance, a customer buying anti-hairfall oil might apply it for only three days instead of the recommended six weeks. Educating them patiently builds immense trust.

4. Offer a Structured Resolution (Refund, Replacement, or Expert Consultation)

If the product genuinely failed due to a manufacturing defect, or if it simply wasn't the right fit for their specific skin/car type/hair texture, stand behind your business ethics.

Indian consumers deeply value fairness. If you offer a replacement batch, store credit, or a complimentary consultation with your in-house expert (like a dermatologist in your clinic or a senior mechanic in your auto shop), you turn a detractor into a loyal advocate.

Once the issue is amicably resolved on WhatsApp, you can gently request them to update their Google review to reflect how professionally you handled their concern.
